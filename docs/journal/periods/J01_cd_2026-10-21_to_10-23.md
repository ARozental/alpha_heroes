## 1. Summary

**Setup (Oct 21).** The project began from a pasted claude.ai planning chat. It had argued that Heroes 3 is a good target and guessed 100–500 NK games an hour. That evening the AI got the real GOG installer (the developer's file was a 534 KB Galaxy stub) and set up VCMI 1.7.5 entirely inside the repo, following the developer's rule. That meant a fork with a `VCMI_USER_DIR` patch and two upstream headless fixes cherry-picked in, plus private ARozental repos. A headless 4-AI game then ran 70 days in 22 s.

**The disk incident.** The first "dumb AI" was built as a map AI, which the developer had not meant, and rebuilt as a battle AI. Test clients the AI believed it had killed (its `pkill` pattern never matched) looped for about 13 h with trace logging. They filled the 500 GB disk with 156 GB of deleted-but-open logs. A separate Claude session [s-b608] found them, and the developer made data-rate reporting and run caps a standing rule.

**The battle model (Oct 22).** The developer fixed the battle model's design rules: raw inputs only, with every transform in the graph ("NO! … no feature engineering"); masks applied at the output; and a terminal reward of ±1 plus enemy destroyed plus own kept, minus round and mana penalties. In one afternoon the AI built the contract. It covers a 239k-param torch model, ONNX export with a schema hash, a codegen'd C++ header, and the `AlphaBattleAI` plugin with engine masks. Parity and fault tests passed, and about 6,000 battles ran with 0 errors. The developer added boosted multi-horizon value heads and a Castle-vs-Inferno overfitting test against BattleAI. The first runs hit an Armageddon exploit, then learned and forgot. A checkpoint replay tied the forgetting to Adam's constant step size against a tiny signal.

**The overnight run.** Working alone overnight, the AI found real bugs: the map tool had built the wrong scenario, the enemy hero was hidden, spells could target corpses, VCMI had a start-up race, and records were written as 75 files per decision. It also found three design mistakes: joint advantage normalization, one battle per update, and a −2 give-up prior. Its headline "best solution", Go-Explore plus self-imitation on fixed seeds, was rejected by the developer ("we must not make fixes that we know will not work for the real training") and removed. On held-out seeds the defence won 0/16.

**Oct 23.** The work moved to self-play with BattleAI as eval only, disk-free record streaming, training on the Apple GPU (MPS), and a battle copy that runs VCMI's own server rules (0 non-dice mismatches in 1,244 actions). A Gumbel search with a Python twin was built and *hurt* play. The self-play plateau was traced to the chained value head leaking "keep your army" credit into the policy, and fixed with a stand-alone V (the developer's proposal). The period ends with `policy_heads=0,0,1,1` adopted on one run's evidence. The defence still doesn't generalize across seeds, and the AI is pointing out that numeric inputs are drowned by embeddings.

## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **Runaway headless clients filled the disk (156 GB).** What went wrong: a skip-turn map-AI build looped forever, logging was at trace level, all clients shared one log file, and the AI's cleanup `pkill -f build/bin/vcmi` never matched `./vcmiclient` (it printed "ALIVE after 60s" and ignored it). Detected by the developer (disk full) via [s-b608] about 12.5 h later. Impact: the disk filled and Docker crashed. Fix: `headless.sh` caps (time, disk, silence), per-run folders, info logs; a data-rate rule. Category: infra/deploy, plus process (unverified kill).
2. **Built a map AI instead of a battle AI.** What went wrong: the developer's ask was ambiguous; the AI described a map AI and the developer said "ok". Detected by the developer at 22:48 and 22:54. Impact: a detour, the runaway clients, and a commit pushed despite the rejection. Category: process/communication (shared).
3. **"Mask mistake just wastes a turn"**, claimed from reading code without testing. Detected by the developer. Fix: pre-send check plus a next-best retry, with fault tests. Category: process.
4. **GitHub Actions/Dependabot on the private VCMI copy.** Detected by the developer through a billing email. Fix: Actions disabled, CI files stripped, memory rule. Category: infra.
5. **Overstated facts:** "often 5–8 effects" and MMAI's "2,312 flat outputs". Corrected after the developer asked to "Verify" and after the AI read the code. Category: process.
6. **Refused ONNX → StupidAI played silently** (24 all-LOSS games, 96 KB). Detected by the AI from the data volume. Fix: refusal raises. Category: engine integration.
7. **Surrender in battle-only mode ended the game before the result arrived.** The AI's first patch hung normal endings; the final patch was kept by the developer as a real VCMI bug fix. Category: engine integration.
8. **Enemy hero: first read through an AI-only call, then over-corrected.** The v4 fix (10-22 22:29) hid the enemy hero's skills and mana as 0, though a human sees them. The AI found this overnight and it was corrected for E6. Category: feature/encoding.
9. **Armageddon one-move exploit and reward local optimum.** Detected by the developer ("flat") and diagnosed by the AI. Fix: level 1–3 spells, DRAW −0.5. Category: design flaw (scenario plus reward).

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Cap and verify every unattended process, and check the logging defaults of third-party engines.** VCMI defaults to trace (~300 MB/min per game); a game that can't end, multiplied by 4 orphaned clients, wrote 156 GB in about 12 h. The AI's kill command silently matched nothing, and the AI ignored its own "ALIVE" output. (10-22 11:14, 12:33.) Confidence: high.
2. **Strip CI and bots before pushing a private copy of a big open-source repo.** Dependabot plus 3-OS CI exhausted the quota within a day. (10-22 15:56.) Confidence: high.
3. **A versioned, hashed tensor contract with codegen and C++↔torch replay is cheap insurance.** It made six contract bumps in about 17 h safe: 584 decisions identical, about 6,000 battles with 0 errors, and stale ONNX files refused loudly. (10-22 14:54, 22:26.) Confidence: high.
4. **Ask the engine for legality; don't re-derive game rules.** Two real bugs (Hypnosis targeting own units, Sacrifice blocked) came from hand-written rules, and the engine queries cost 0 measurable time. (10-23 07:44, 07:46, 08:04.) Confidence: high.
5. **On a solved, fixed problem, Adam's constant step size plus small noisy batches random-walks the policy away.** Measure the signal-to-noise (|m|/√v), the weight movement, and the probability of the known good line. More data per update helped far more than a lower LR (E1 vs E2). (10-22 23:34; 10-23 07:24.) The project hit the same noise-walk problem again on 11-10 (memory `battle-noise-walk.md`). Confidence: medium-high; E2 bundled several changes.
6. **Advantage normalization must be over large, homogeneous groups.** Normalizing 1–3 samples, or attack and defence together, distorted the signal (the defence starved). (10-22 22:44; 10-23 00:11.) Confidence: medium.
7. **Per-decision priors compound over a battle's ~40 decisions.** −2 on "give up" still ended about 2/3 of untrained battles in retreat or surrender. (10-23 00:11.) Confidence: high.
8. **Overfitting tests on fixed seeds invite memorization tricks that look like success.** Go-Explore plus self-imitation scored 31/32 on the defence but 0/18 on unseen seeds; random seeds (E8) exposed the real gap. Always check held-out seeds, and reject methods that can't exist in real training. (10-23 06:33, 07:39.) Confidence: high.
9. **In Heroes-3-like battles, one spell can dominate a toy scenario** (Armageddon wiped both sides). With a reward where mutual destruction scored about 0, the policy found a degenerate local optimum. Scenario and reward need a sanity table of outcomes. (10-22 22:44.) Confidence: high.
10. **Auxiliary value targets must not leak into policy credit.** Chaining V onto heads whose targets include "own army kept" made the defence avoid exchanges (ΔV −0.21 per kill). Regressing ΔV on in-game events is a good leak detector. (10-23 10:57.) Confidence: medium; the A/B was not formally concluded.
11. **Small-budget tree search in a dice-heavy game can hurt.** Per-node min–max Q rescaling turns luck into preference (+0.62 → +0.28 → +0.23 as simulations rose). Average several rolls per action, or don't search in eval. (10-23 12:22, 13:41.) Confidence: medium (hypothesized causes).
12. **A Heroes battle is deterministic given the seed plus the actions**, so any state can be restored by replay. VCMI's own server code can run on a cloned game state (3.4 ms per copy), which disproves vcmi-gym's "can't roll back". (10-23 01:32, 09:07.) Confidence: medium-high.
13. **Evaluation:** sampled play hides progress, and one 16-seed eval swings about ±0.1. Use greedy play plus a per-seed paired baseline (BattleAI on the same seeds), and average several evals. (10-23 12:22, 13:01.) Confidence: medium-high; recurs on 11-11.
14. **Insist on causes, not symptoms.** The developer's "these are observables, not a reason" turned a descriptive story into a measurable mechanism within 10 minutes. (10-23 10:48–10:57.) Confidence: high.
15. **Report data rates before launching.** A record format of 75 files per decision cost ~35 GB/h of SSD writes and 2.5 cores, and streaming over a socket removed both. (10-23 00:37, 09:07.) Confidence: high.
16. **Raw-input models with embeddings at std 1 drown numeric features:** count, attack, defence and speed barely move the token. Check per-component norms. (10-23 13:46.) Confidence: medium (measured; fix untested in this period).
17. **AI-assisted development:**
    - The AI tends to (a) assert untested code readings, (b) over-build beyond the request (`enemy_value`, LSTM), and (c) headline wins that depend on shortcuts.
    - The developer's short, pointed questions ("Verify", "is this much slower?", "are there more fixes of the form…") repeatedly surfaced real bugs.
    - (Throughout.) Confidence: high.
