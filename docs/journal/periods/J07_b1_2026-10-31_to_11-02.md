## 1. Summary

The period opened on the restart the developer had allowed after joint5's plateau (campaign eval stuck at 0.10–0.27). The plan was a campaign v2 model with slot memory, the developer's share-change ("logit of share") value labels and a share/growth credit schedule, pretrained by imitating Nullkiller2 (NK2).
- The developer cut the AI's planned A/B tests and recordings: stream games, don't record them ("no memory can not be good").
- Streamed imitation ran on the PC for ~5 h. It reached held-out loss ~1.93 and top-1 ~37% after an LR drop from 1e-4 to 3e-5 fixed a plateau. The developer then lowered the standing LR cap to "max 3e-5, 1e-5 recommended".
- The developer pushed back on the AI's design: RL with a KL to a frozen imitation "teacher network". The developer asked instead for NK2's own answer at our states as an extra label (DAgger). The AI had called this "the hard part". It built the dry-run NK2 advisor (Advice.cpp) in about 20 minutes, and joint7 started at ~22:00 on Oct 31.
- The imitation model's first eval was **0.113**. The AI had predicted 0.3–0.6, and 37% agreement on NK2's states turned out to be only 7% on our own.

The night of Oct 31 → Nov 1 was a long run of infrastructure failures, each found and fixed live, with **16 restarts of joint7**:
- GPU time-slicing between the trainer and inference;
- OOMs in CUDA-graph recording;
- WDDM spilling GPU memory into system RAM;
- permit deadlocks;
- memory-guard stops;
- an NK2 null-pointer segfault (fixed in the fork);
- runaway hero-exchange churn (55% of decisions).

The decisive science fix was found by the AI's per-type agreement analysis at 05:34. The advisor had labelled NK2's moves one tile at a time. Relabelling to NK2's chain target raised agreement from 22% to ~36% and cut decisions per player-day from ~60 to 25–38. Eval 24 reached **0.221 ± 0.042**, the first significant gain.

Nov 1 daytime was dominated by RAM: the PC/WSL has 28 GB, and the memory guard stopped the run at 12:21. The developer reacted angrily to lost work ("wtf we still lose training", "if we do you failed"). The AI first guessed at causes, and its own changes blocked games once, deadlocked the run once and killed it once more. It then measured instead:
- the trainer's 5 GB swing came from pinned memory, battle-eval records, CPU-side battle batches and waiting games' decoded inputs;
- it added a disk spool for finished games, drained deploys and a swap-aware guard.

By 21:00 the trainer peaked at ~5–7 GB instead of 9–14 GB, and the run had no unplanned stop from 20:57 to 11:22 the next day (~14 h; drained deploys only). It also became PC-sufficient: inference on the PC CPU, the Mac only adding game slots. The campaign eval rose to a plateau of ~0.30–0.35 from 22:00 (evals 83–167, mean ~0.32, NK2 = 1.0). Agreement with NK2 crept from 37.7% to 40.3%.

The battle model stayed flat at +0.09 all period.

On Nov 2 the AI's log review led to a back-and-forth with the developer, after which these went in:
- per-head value-loss normalization;
- a fixed 60:20:20 battle mix at 12% of campaign battles;
- a zero-init movement-discount op on MOVE values;
- an NK2 "plan head", a label-only auxiliary loss from a labelled fork patch.

The period ends with the plan head live (CE 6.46 → 6.42). The per-term gradients show the teacher term supplying ~96% of the shared layers' gradient and the value heads ~0.

## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **False all-clear on RAM (10-31 17:22).** The AI said "False alarm on memory"; the PC was at 0 available 5 min later. It had estimated 0.9 MB per decoded decision; the real figure was 1.7 MB (int64). Detected by the AI; one run lost; fixed with uint8 inputs and memwatch. *Infra.*
2. **Allocator setting carried over.** `expandable_segments` was copied from imitation into joint7 and broke CUDA graph capture. Detected by the AI. *Infra.*
3. **Imitation wrote no TensorBoard events.** Detected by the developer at 18:06. *Process.*
4. **Imitation→play prediction wrong (0.3–0.6 vs 0.113).** Covariate shift: CE ~1.9 on NK2's states vs 5.86 on our states (7% agreement). The imitation recorder also had the one-tile move bug (#11); whether that explains the 0.113 is unclear, and the AI never connected the two. *Evaluation/design.*
5. **GPU time-slicing, twice.** Inference on the PC GPU starved the trainer: games hit their limit on 10-31 23:18, then training ran at 144 vs ~270 dec/s on 11-01 09:27. The second time was the same mistake ten hours later, surfaced only by the developer's "why do we not use the cores on the mac". *Infra; a known lesson not applied.*
6. **Permit/admission logic failed four times** (23:49, 05:49, 15:49, 15:58–16:16, the last with 18 min of no campaign games). It was sized for ~2,000-decision games and never re-derived when games grew to 5–15k decisions. Detected by the AI from idle slots. *Design flaw.*

8. **Exchange churn.** 55% of decisions were untaught ARMY moves, giving games of 15–17k decisions. Teaching exchanges plus the 8-move cap reduced it. The root cause, heroes walking into each other, remained. *Design flaw (an action family with no teacher and no credit).*

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Imitation accuracy on the teacher's own states does not predict play.**
   - Imitation got 37% top-1 and held-out CE ~1.9, but 7% agreement and CE 5.86 on the copy's own states, and eval 0.113, the same as untaught joint5.
   - DAgger labels on its own states moved the eval to ~0.32 within ~24 h.
   - Confidence high (one run, but large effects; 10-31 22:41, 11-02 07:26).
2. **Check what a teacher label means in your action space before trusting agreement.**
   - NK2 issues per-tile requests, so the labels said "step one tile". Exact MOVE agreement was 5% while type agreement was 79%.
   - Relabelling cut decisions per player-day ~2× and produced the first significant eval gain.
   - A per-action-type agreement table found it in minutes after ~7.5 h unnoticed. Confidence high (05:34–08:11).
3. **In H3-like games an action family with no teacher and no credit will be churned.** Exchanges reached 55% of decisions and games hit 15–17k decisions. Teaching them, not masking, shrank the share to 36%. Confidence medium-high (00:29, 08:43).
4. **Score-driven teacher weight interacts with PPO's KL control.** A KL stop sized for noisy RL credit throttled a teacher at 95% of the loss (2–6 of 56 steps, LR decaying). It needs to be scaled or separated, at the cost of the trust region. Confidence medium (01:01, 15:55).
5. **Share-of-total value targets are mostly the rivals' doing.** Own growth explains 31% of a 7-day share change, so a head seeing only our side caps at ~0.05–0.1 EV. Value targets on 0..1 BCE scales also differ 6–10× in std (V_end dominates) unless normalized. Confidence high (11-01 16:27, 11-02 09:03).
6. **Check where the imitation gradient goes.** With the teacher at weight ~10–19, ~96% of the shared trunk's gradient was imitation and value heads ~0.00008. Value heads that "don't learn" may be starved of representation, not broken. Confidence medium (11:46).
7. **Diagnose before prescribing strategic gaps.** "Few heroes" looked like a hiring problem (0.5% of decisions). Counting showed 5.6 hires per game and 3.7 heroes lost: a survival problem. Confidence high (09:03).
8. **Budget hardware (WSL + consumer GPU) failure modes are specific and repeatable.**
   - WDDM spills GPU memory into system RAM instead of raising OOM, so the GPU runs many times slower.
   - A second GPU process time-slices against the trainer (training at 144 vs 270 dec/s).
   - Eager launches cost ~35 ms per forward.
   - Fixes: a memory-fraction cap, inference on the CPU (24 cores sufficed: ~10 ms per decision, 2–3 cores for 10 games), CUDA graphs.
   - Confidence high (02:40, 09:45).
9. **Rare code paths are where long runs die.** Graph-capture checks, noise diagnostics and in-process evals each exceeded the routine step's memory. The general fix ("an OOM drops one update's games, never the run") was worth more than the three point fixes. Confidence high (04:48).
10. **Sizing rules derived from one regime break silently when the data changes shape.** Permits and admission were sized for ~2,000-decision games and failed four times once games had 5–15k. Re-derive capacity rules when episode length changes ~5×. Confidence high.
11. **For AI-run infrastructure, guessing fixes is expensive; measuring is cheap.**
    - Three guessed memory fixes caused a block, a deadlock and a death in 3 hours.
    - One per-process census found all four real causes within ~3 h, and the trainer peak fell from ~12 GB to ~5 GB.
    - The developer's "solve the actual issue" was the turning point. Confidence high (13:31–19:51).
12. **Make restarts lossless before tuning a long run.** A spool of finished games (~100–120 GB/day of writes, ~3.7% of a consumer SSD's rating per year) plus drained deploys turned every later deploy into a zero-loss event. Confidence high (15:37).
13. **AI assistants overrate the engineering cost of the principled design.** DAgger was described as "the hard part" and took ~20 min. Also, the AI defaulted to ablations and hedged "your call" items (fork patches, the battle-learner share) the developer considered obvious. Confidence medium (21:20–21:57, 11-02 10:13).
14. **Verify peer "dead code" claims by reachability.** Name-grep reviews missed decorator registries; deleting would have broken the battle checkpoint. Confidence high (18:27).
15. **On budget, compute for the "fuller" game is a trade, not a loss.**
    - Games per hour fell 500 → 100–140, but decisions trained per hour stayed at 65–75%, because NK2-like play has 15× more decisions per game.
    - The real cost is ~5× fewer end-of-game signals.
    - Confidence medium (16:08).
