## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Test a diagnosis against the event before building on it.** The raw value loss tracked the batch's target variance (corr 0.98–0.99). An 8× loss jump was 0.82→0.88 unexplained, so a valve keyed on raw loss responds to eliminations in the batch, not to critic quality. Evidence 09:22–09:28. High confidence.
2. **With few outcome labels per game (~3), a shared trunk shaped by dense per-decision losses gives better value features than a separate value trunk.** Held-out EV: frozen shared 0.369 vs own trunk 0.226–0.321. This is evidence against PPG-style splits at a budget scale of 50–300 games. Evidence 08:36 and 11:25 (partial). Medium-high confidence at this data scale.
3. **Measure the teacher's worth inside your own action space before arguing about distillation.** "Play its matched choice" cost ~50 min and gave +0.29 ± 0.06. It showed the gap was knowledge, not action mapping, and that 3,000 updates of imitation loss had captured little of it: agreement 0.24–0.26 against 0.92 when followed. Evidence 10:12. High confidence.
4. **Audit that schedules actually act.** A frozen loss-share setting silently cancelled the teacher fade for ~5 days, at 60–65% of the trunk gradient. Evidence 08:54, 11:18. High confidence.
5. **Zero-init weights on a new feature are effectively dead at lr 1e-5 with Adam.** Measured: 0.1–0.5 logits/day; fight weights ~0.005 after 3,000 updates; flipping the fight forecast moved MOVE by KL 0.00004. A feature needs a fixed-sign start (a prior), an offline-fitted start, or a faster step. Evidence 11:57, 16:40, 18:05. High confidence.
6. **A logit prior is a weak proxy for "do what the planner says".** The top candidate's prior raised following to 0.33–0.36. A logit bump on its choice cannot outvote our own negative priors (trades taken 20%). Only an explicit mixture reproduced the +0.29. Evidence 12:12, 12:44, 13:58. Medium-high confidence.
7. **Planner-in-the-loop play (mixture) buys score, not learning, and creates incoherent control.**
   - The exact gradient on followed rows shrinks by ×P_own/9.
   - The headline eval measures the planner.
   - The two controllers undo each other: NK2 parks defenders and our 10% walks them out, so towns lost went 23→100.
   - Self-play eliminations tripled (0.16 vs 0.05).

   For Heroes-like games, partial control by a scripted AI breaks multi-turn plans that only one agent understands. Evidence 13:35, 13:42, 17:17. Medium-high confidence.
8. **Heterogeneous game types need separate advantage normalization.** Hidden-choice games' advantage was 0.2–0.6 of the pooled sd, so they were underweighted exactly where learning was wanted. Evidence 17:25. Medium confidence. adv_by_kind was false again at deploy 60; the reason is not visible in this period.
9. **Eliminations in Heroes-like play come from being attacked, not from attacking.** We lose 98.5–99.1% of the battles where we are attacked. In 10 of 13 and 10 of 11 eliminated games the strongest army was lost to an attack on us. Following won 98% of its own attacks at a median 3.13× army ratio. Defence and positioning is the lever. Evidence 16:47. Medium confidence (Mac games, one checkpoint).
10. **Check a capability from the legal-action masks before building it.** The "teleport gap" was a misnamed bucket; masks showed 100% and 98% capability in minutes. Evidence 17:18. High confidence.
11. **One 48-game eval is ±0.11–0.13.** Three replicates side by side twice turned an apparent slide into noise (−0.014 ± 0.033; −0.038 ± 0.042). Judge only paired, replicated arms. Evidence 07:03, 09:18, 11:46. High confidence.
12. **Deploy gates should compare configured values exactly and learned values within bounds, and be dry-run on a copy of the live checkpoint.** CUDA-graph capture breaks when a check keeps an autograd tensor alive, and this recurred. Evidence 13:08, 14:59. High confidence.
13. **AI-assisted development failure mode: measuring instead of building.** The developer had to cap experiments at 10 minutes, then demand that each experiment have an action attached. Under the cap the AI swung to deploying an untested, poorly understood mechanism. Concretely: run a 2-minute mechanism check (does the prior make us follow?) and a settings diff against the reference test before any long test. Evidence 12:04, 12:45, 18:17. High confidence.
14. **The AI needed the developer to get feature design right.** The developer had to insist on how each number is represented (bounded, on hero/tile pairs, multiple value views, one-sided danger), even after earlier memory rules on prior-anchored features. Write a representation table before any feature plan. Evidence 10:07, 10:25. High confidence.
15. **Peer review between two AI sessions catches a lot.** b1 corrected e5 at least 6 times in one day (valve r, Adam, a8b587e, the 0.65 ceiling, mixture gradient, walk-out hypothesis). Verbatim relays of the developer's words helped. Peers also make coordination errors: file overwrites, clock drift, stronger or weaker restatements of the developer's instructions. Medium-high confidence.
