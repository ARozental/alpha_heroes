## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **One YES token for every yes/no question** (design flaw). Found by the AI after the developer's 18:19 questions. Long-lived: since the v2 option head; exact age unclear. Impact: a learned NO lean across dwellings, guards and banks; a 53-refusal loop. Fixed 909067b. *Design flaw / feature encoding.*
2. **Hashed option identities collided** (546/954 rows shared). Detected by **e5** (19:09) and fixed in the same deploy. *Feature encoding.*
3. **Bias-dominated keys** (cosine 0.997). Caught by the AI's own distinctness test before deploy. *Feature encoding.*
4. **Schema-12 trade purchases gave away ~5,200 worth each** (~150k/game, about half of end Φ). The AI's own bug, found by its own measurement ~1.5 h after deploy. Fixed by E (00:38). *Reward/credit (prior pricing).*
5. **Advice-label collapse.** The advice replaced NK2's label at ~40% of decisions; the model gave it P ≈ 0.0002, and CE ~8.5 on those decisions pushed shared type parameters globally.
   - Warning signs were ignored at 01:12 (two KL-stopped updates); detected by the eval about 40 minutes later.
   - Three advice deploys within 35 minutes, unattended, on the strength of "continue fixing … I'll come back tomorrow".
   - Rolled back 01:46. *Training dynamics / process.*
6. **GPU benchmark beside the live run** stalled the learner (00:12). Caught by the watcher. *Infra.*
7. **GIVE_ALL coin-flip receiver.** The drift alarm caught it in 3 updates; fixed in ~45 minutes. Lesson recorded: a new action changes the teacher's labels too (NK2 ARMY labels 52% → 85%). *Design flaw.*
8. **GATHER emptied towns.** Two causes: Φ paid at once while town losses landed days later, and the accept set taught GATHER.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Imitation-label edits are the most dangerous lever in a teacher-guided RL run.** Replacing the teacher's label at ~40% of decisions collapsed the policy in a few updates (eval 0.378 → 0.248). "Accept sets" are not neutral: −log Σp teaches every accepted entry in proportion to its probability (GATHER 0.06 → 0.33 in 7 updates). Restricting the teacher's normaliser to an action family drifted town behaviour in 3 updates. Run a type-drift check (two checkpoints, same recorded inputs) one or two updates after *any* teacher change. Evidence 01:48, 10:41, 17:03. Confidence high.
2. **A new action changes what the teacher labels, even with no advice change.** GIVE_ALL in random directions flipped NK2's exchange labels from 52% to 85% ARMY. Measure the teacher's label mix per decision kind after adding actions. Evidence 08:45. Confidence high.
3. **Macro-actions shorten credit chains but make mispricing catastrophic.** In one day:
   - GIVE_ALL ping-pong;
   - GATHER emptying towns (garrisons 80% → 21%);
   - DROP destroying ~12k gold of creatures a game;
   - TAKE_BEST stranding creatures.

   Every macro needs (a) its risk priced, not just its gain, (b) a mismatch counter between the priced and the realised result, and (c) a same-weights A/B against the reference AI before the run trains on it. Evidence 08:45–19:59. Confidence high.
4. **Separate "code bug" from "weight drift" with paired same-code, different-weights mini-evals.** 24 setups gave decisive answers (0.390 vs 0.274; 0.394 vs 0.235) when single PC evals (SE ~0.055–0.065 on 48 games) could not. Evidence 10:41, 19:59. Confidence high.
5. **Never couple a training knob to one noisy eval.** The score-driven teacher weight moved 3.5× across a noise-sized swing, loosened the anchor after a lucky 0.476, and led to a 0.297 slide. Evidence 03:44. Confidence high.
6. **Shaping-reward edits are costly.** Each Φ change cost ~100 updates of value fit (growth EV 0.45 → 0.24), and a fixed-share loss balancer throttles the value term exactly when targets move. Four Φ edits in three days compounded this. Batch Φ changes, and let the value share rise after label changes. Evidence 10:16, 18:42. Confidence medium-high.
7. **Audit features by failure case.** The neutral-town bug was invisible for weeks. It surfaced only by taking the worst outcomes (hopeless attacks) and asking what the features said about each ("no fight" in 17/17 cases). Engine helper semantics (`getTownsInfo(false)` = only players' towns; `guardingCreaturePosition` flags fogged guards) are a common source. Evidence 23:07. Confidence high.
8. **Legality mismatches between the encoder and the server silently teach "this action is useless".** Examples: 569 BUILD SHIP failures, 51 Town Portal refusals. Mirror the server's rules from visible state only (Summon Boat: no hidden-information legality). Evidence 13:20, 13:15. Confidence high.
9. **The developer's demand that an explanation must predict the failure in advance is a good filter.** The AI's "targets changed" story was falsified by its own test within 30 minutes once the developer insisted (Φ variants all had std 0.132). Evidence 07:38–08:10. Confidence high.

Two loss changes 40 minutes apart produced a KL burst that looked like a bug. Rule: one change, then at least one clean eval (the developer later wrote this down, per memory). Evidence 15:03, 17:59. Confidence high.

Benchmarks beside the run OOM-risk the learner. Gate deploys with an on-GPU capture check. Evidence 20:55, 00:12. Confidence high.

Imitation taught the opposite of NK2's behaviour (P(take garrison) = 0.0002; heroes led 2% of the army at day 60). Check which teacher behaviours are invisible to the label pipeline. Evidence 07:05. Confidence high.
13. **Compressed priors (value_logit) under-penalise catastrophes.** Losing a third of an army scored only about −3 logits, so ~3% per decision, and evals sampling at temperature 1 replay those errors. Usually-useless destructive actions need strongly negative starts (developer's rule). Evidence 17:59, 19:59. Confidence medium-high.
14. **"Frozen" is not "converged".** The battle eval spread of ±0.004 over 53 evals came from 4 optimizer steps per update at KL 5e-5. The config's own target was never checked. The fix was then judged by a metric whose noise baseline was not established; e5 later found it wrong. After a recipe change, compare signal metrics to a pure-noise baseline. Evidence 13:25, 14:14; later 11-10. Confidence high.
