## 1. Summary

The period opened with **joint2** running on the Mac: the v1 campaign model plus the battle model, trained jointly against themselves and evaluated against Nullkiller2 (NK2), BattleAI and MMAI. The battle eval sat at a deterministic ~+0.08. The campaign eval vs NK2 was flat at ~0.12–0.23 (1.0 = parity with an average player). The AI first called the campaign flat-but-healthy. The developer insisted "something is really off there, and not a monor thing" (10-29 22:00). Max-effort bug hunting then found a VCMI map-writer bug that had stripped every random-map town of its Tavern and dwellings, in all training and eval games. The same hunt found an encoder blind to what tavern heroes bring, and an empire value Φ that priced levels 1–3 at zero and hires negatively. Fixes followed overnight:
- the town bug, hiring inputs, and a skill-based hero Φ;
- a signed hire prior and a reset of a learned "walk onto monsters" move bias;
- a greedy eval tried and abandoned because greedy play oscillated.

Hero counts rose sharply, but the campaign eval stayed at ~0.14 by day 56 (~0.5 at day 28). The battle eval rose to +0.090.

On 10-30 the developer turned to architecture: "i'm pretty sure it is very bad now". A long design dialogue produced the **campaign v2** design. It has typed raw-input encoders, ownership transforms that are not a sign flip, a whole-map U-Net, "foresight" auxiliary heads whose hidden states feed forward, and logits as actor·target matches. The battle model got function-preserving upgrades: foresight on the enemy's reply, a wider context, and acting-unit queries. These kept its level (+0.096/+0.096, saved as `battle_battle3_05829.pt`). A Fable-max review fixed real v2 bugs, but it also added NK2 opponents to training. The developer rejected that as "wrong/cheating" and it became a standing rule. **joint5** (v2 from scratch) started at 15:23 and moved to the RTX 5090 PC that evening. b1's PC evening was chaotic:
- 8 memory-guard stops and 12 process restarts in 2.6 h;
- unanswered status questions;
- a wrong "WSL launch overhead" diagnosis.

The developer stopped b1 ("STOP", 21:12) and gave the speed work to e5. e5 rebuilt the loop overnight; campaign updates went from ~8/h to ~25–36/h.

On 10-31 b1 took the run back. It fixed four pipeline bugs, including battle-learner starvation, and moved the battle learner to large accumulated batches. That first froze it at LR 1e-5; it then ran at 3e-5. b1 also made the campaign update ~20–25% faster. It then made a foreseeable mistake: it **masked info spells** on the strength of a self-play metric, and the eval fell to 0.072. That was reverted, and the developer made "never mask actions" a rule. With the campaign plateaued at ~0.2, the work turned to learnability, on the developer's principle that the model must be able to "replicate/generalize the existing solution". The pieces:
- an NK2-vs-NK2 action audit, which found missing actions;
- a 47% parameter cut by collapsing hashed subtype tables;
- a zero-junk-game guard;
- an "attributed" credit with growth as its backbone (its event term turned out ~2.6% sparse);
- an NK2 teacher recorder with imitation (baseline top-1 4.9%);
- finally, on the developer's demand, a memory module (schema 10).

The period ends mid-build of teacher/memory training (the joint6 prep). The campaign eval stood at ~0.10–0.19 on 48 games.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **A flat learning curve across thousands of games, with failure counts unchanged from the first eval, means "audit the environment", not "it's noise".** The town-builder bug sat under ~2,000 games of training plus the AI's no-collapse statistics. The developer's intuition beat the AI's statistics. Check what the agent's world actually contains, such as starting buildings, before tuning. (10-29 21:52 → 22:28; high confidence.)
2. **For economy games, a value function that prices assets wrongly produces rational but terrible behaviour.** Two examples:
   - Heroes worth 0 at levels 1–3 made suicidal monster fights cheap; the model learned +2.5 log-odds toward monster tiles.
   - Hires priced negatively plus a blind encoder meant no hiring.
   Fixing Φ changed behaviour within hours: hires 0 → 8–14 per game. Write the value table as a readable document (`docs/empire_value.md`) and test it for gaps. (10-29 23:24, 10-30 00:35; high.)
3. **Self-play metrics can say the opposite of the real objective.**
   - Self-play heroes rose while heroes against NK2 fell.
   - Info spells looked like a value exploit in self-play but were essential against an aggressive opponent (eval 0.22 → 0.07).
   - Self-play opponents rarely attack (1–3 battles in 21 days).
   Always judge behaviour changes against the external benchmark, one change at a time. (10-29 21:52, 10-31 10:42; high.)
4. **Greedy decoding of a policy trained with sampling can be a different, broken policy.** With a spread-out move distribution whose argmax is a neighbouring tile, greedy play oscillated (95% one-tile moves). Use seeded sampling for paired evals. (10-30 02:16; high.)
5. **Low auxiliary losses on rare targets are mostly base rate.** Always compare to the base-rate loss. Here only 1 of 4 battle foresight heads clearly learned (68% better; the others 6–14%). (10-30 14:16; high.)
6. **Model upgrades can be made function-preserving with zero-init paths.** The battle model kept +0.096 through new heads and a wider context, saving days of retraining. (10-30 14:51; high.)
7. **"Bigger batch, smaller LR" must be checked against steps per update and KL.** 12× batch at LR 1e-5 with 3 steps per update froze the battle model (KL 2e-5). Going from 3,072 to 6,144 decisions didn't raise the signal ratio, because the remaining noise is between updates (policy drift, data mix), not within them. (10-31 12:38, 12:59; medium-high.)
8. **For a small team, measure throughput in isolation before theorizing.** b1's per-op-overhead story was wrong; WDDM time-slicing among GPU processes was the cause. A WSL-specific trap: several CUDA processes on one Windows GPU time-slice. Inference as a thread in the trainer was 12× slower because of the GIL; e5 moved inference to CPUs and to the Mac instead. (10-30 22:30; high.)
9. **Pipeline invariants need cheap always-on validators.**
   - Inference outages silently produced 6,321 "finished" games with no decisions.
   - A per-process counter hid ~90% of games being discarded.
   - Idle clients killed by a watchdog caused most battle failures.
   - A filter (decisions per player-day ≥ 0.5; clean games ≥ 2.76) separates cleanly.
   (10-31 13:39, 10-30 21:03, 10-31 09:11; high.)
10. **On a shared remote machine, restarts are expensive.** 12 restarts in 157 minutes yielded 77 minutes of training. Batch fixes; deploy fewer, verified changes. (10-30 21:03; high.)
11. **Learnability audit before RL.** Recording NK2's requests showed actions our space can't express. GarrisonHeroSwap alone (228 in 397 player-turns) outnumbers hires, and in-army stack arranging is 1,532. The teacher idea forces this audit. (10-31 12:59; high.)
12. **Capacity placement matters more than total size.**
    - Hashed subtype tables held 1.1M dead parameters.
    - In the 1.18M v2 model, the reasoning trunk is 67k (6%) and input tables ~70%.
    - Most early behaviour came from hand priors.
    (10-31 12:38, 15:02; medium.)
13. **Credit in a long multiplayer economy game is dominated by shared luck.**
    - 72% of advantage variance is common to all of a player's decisions that day.
    - The gradient noise scale under the current credit was ∞ (no measurable signal) after day 14.
    - Own-growth targets are cleaner early (1,310 decisions vs 2,979 for share in days 1–14).
    - Event-attributed opponent damage is too sparse (2.6% of decisions) to carry weight early.
    (10-29 22:28, 10-31 14:12; medium; the noise numbers are single readings.)
14. **Eval noise:** a 24-game campaign eval sampled at temperature 1 resolves only about ±0.1; 48 games roughly ±0.05. Per-machine map generation and restart stalls corrupt comparisons unless the maps are pinned and stalled games are excluded. (10-29 21:52, 10-31 10:32; high.)
15. **Reviews by a stronger model and by subagents find real bugs, but can override the developer's intent.** The Fable review caught the type-score bug and the noisy random-init heads, and also added NK2 opponents. The subagent found 10 issues in a day's rebuild. Give reviewers the standing rules. (10-30 15:17, 10-31 14:23; high.)
16. **Answer the developer's direct questions even mid-task.** Unanswered status questions over 2.5 h ended in the task being reassigned. (10-30 18:23–22:35; high.)
