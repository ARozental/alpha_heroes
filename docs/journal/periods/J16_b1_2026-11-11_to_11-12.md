## 1. Summary

The night began with a small deploy (61: the developer's action price at 1e-5). It then turned into a three-way reversal of the training regime. Two lookups b1 ran for e5 showed weaker optimizer readings after deploy 60 made every game own-against-own: trunk signal fraction 0.066 → 0.018, value group's signal to noise, KL doubled, RECRUIT per player-day −21%. On those proxies e5 rolled the run back to v3205 on the mixed-games line (deploy 62, 23:47). The developer first questioned whether that brought NK2's decisions back, then agreed ("ok, we can continue with Mixed games"). Within the hour, direct play measurements contradicted the proxies. On the Mac, v3226 (all-own) beat v3205 by +0.148 ± 0.067, and a v3229 replay scored 0.861. On the PC, v3205 evaluated at 0.629 against v3216's 0.768. So deploy 63 (00:35) moved the run forward again to the all-own line from v3229. The developer's overnight mandate ("I want to have it all in the model that trains for the night") was to get a head that predicts the NK2 planner's output into training. Its PC time gate failed twice (1.41, 1.38). b1 diagnosed that the gate timed one-off CUDA-graph recording, and e5 then found a real bug behind it: the head's graph was rebuilt every update. With both fixed, deploy 65 went live at 01:36, and the head learned (AUC 0.5 → 0.92, plan cross-entropy 1.98 against the uniform's 3.70).

From 02:00 to 05:00 b1 ran e5's "stage 2" programme, which asked whether the planner can leave serving. The results were mostly negative but informative. A "late-only" injection recovers almost nothing (R ≈ 0.02). An ablation decomposition showed the policy uses the planner mainly as a **candidate marker on reachable MOVE cells** (91% of destinations are the cell itself; same-cell alone recovers R 0.74 of MOVE's 0.81), not as routing. That killed e5's routing hypothesis and with it the w_stop engine feature b1 had just built (stop agreement 0.09). The planner's candidate set is locally predictable (AUC 0.96–0.98), but its pick is not (top-1 ≈ 0.30, recall 0.18 at precision 0.75). In games on v3255, live / blind / move_live / gate_pred all fell within noise (0.653–0.730, se ≈ 0.06–0.07). A harness mismatch, followed by a wait-loop bug that silently lost 2h20m (pgrep self-match), delayed the decisive replays until 07:05.

The morning replays overturned the night's headline. The same v3229 weights that scored 0.861 replayed at **0.697** (−0.164 ± 0.080). On the Mac, one 48-game arm moves about ±0.08 run to run, so the Mac shows no fall from v3229 to v3289 and the "all-own is clearly better" evidence had rested partly on a lucky arm. Meanwhile the PC's evals fell three times in a row: 0.836 → 0.787 → 0.629 → 0.583. A rollback (deploy 66, to v3254) was prepared and deliberately not run. When the developer returned, they suspected the opposite of the night's conclusion: the mixed games gave most of the gain, and the all-own gain was optimizer memory. So deploy 67 (back to mixed games, plus a precision-weighted fix to the credit segments' F statistic found while disproving e5's calendar hypothesis) was scripted and dry-run. At 08:50 the run was on deploy 65 and replicates of v3254 and v3205 were queued to decide the regime. Two new input features, F3 (town holding) and F1 (other heroes' time to a cell), were drafted but untested.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **One 48-game eval arm is not a data point you can chain into a series.** The same weights scored 0.861 and 0.697 (−0.164 ± 0.080); v3301 scored 0.634 and 0.775; v3255 scored 0.632 / 0.737 / 0.805. One regime decision and one headline to the developer rested on single arms. Rule: at least 2 replicates, each run alone, before comparing checkpoints. Evidence: 07:58, 08:49. *High confidence.*
2. **Eval-host load biases scores, not just timings.** v3255 read 0.685 at load about 11 against 0.805 at load 5–8 (+0.121 ± 0.086, a hint at 1.4 se). On a budget the eval machine is also the dev machine, so only arms run under matched conditions are comparable. Evidence: 07:22. *Medium.*
3. **Optimizer-state proxies are contaminated for several updates after a regime change.** Trunk signal fraction went 0.126 → 0.077 → 0.032 over deploy 62's first three updates, and ratio_body went 0.28 → 0.13 within about 6 updates (e5). A rollback triggered by such proxies (deploy 62) was contradicted by play within an hour. Judge regime changes by play, measured with replicates, not by gradient statistics. Evidence: 00:16, 08:12. *Medium-high.*
4. **Gate steady-state cost, not first-use cost.** A one-update timing check included CUDA-graph recording (first-update ratio 1.278 against steady 1.027). The same misread also hid a real caching bug. Evidence: 01:08–01:30. *High.*
5. **KL-ratio ablations localise an auxiliary input's use in minutes.** R = 1 − KL(live‖mode)/KL(live‖blind) per consumer showed that toward carries R 0.81 while the threat/tier layers carry 0. But R measures closeness to the planner-following policy, not quality: gate_pred had R(MOVE) ≈ 0 and played level with live. Use R to choose what to test in games, never as the verdict. Evidence: 02:17, 03:46, 04:43. *Medium-high.*
6. **What a policy takes from a scripted planner can be much simpler than assumed.** Here it was a "candidate here" marker on cells reachable today (90.6% same-cell), not routing. The candidate set was locally predictable (AUC 0.96–0.98), but the planner's pick was not (top-1 ≈ 0.30; recall 0.18 at precision 0.75): the pick needs the planner's global evaluation across heroes and the map. Replacing a strong scripted AI's inputs with local predictions recovers the easy part only. Evidence: 02:26, 03:46, 03:56. *Medium* (one checkpoint, 1,011 decisions).
7. **Dependence on a privileged input deepens with training.** On v3229 the TRAVEL route alone recovered TRAVEL at R 0.85; on v3255, 26 updates later, no single route passed 0.3. If the input must eventually be removed, use input dropout early. Evidence: 03:31. *Low-medium* (two checkpoints, different samples).
8. **The planner's play value was small and unstable when measured.** −0.132 ± 0.078 (v3226) and −0.032 ± 0.075 (v3255), both with the planner-off arm out of distribution. Whether keeping a scripted planner as an input is worth its serving cost was unresolved. Evidence: 23:54, 04:27. *Low.*
9. **Between-group variance statistics that drive learned weights must be precision-weighted.** Otherwise one thin group, ARMY (56–92 game-players against about 600 for the others), dominates. Separately, a fix's measured effect should be checked against the size first claimed: here it was smaller (0.67 → 0.57). Evidence: 08:37–08:41. *Medium.*
10. **A director AI that generates hypotheses plus an executor AI that measures and counts made wrong ideas die fast.** Four hypotheses (routing, calendar, ARMY-thin, late-only) were refuted in one night by minutes-long offline checks. The cheapest decisive check was often a count: 138 of 763; 3,287 of 3,628. *Medium-high.*
11. **Background-job hygiene is a correctness issue, not tidiness.** `pgrep -f` matched its own launcher, nothing verified that the job started, and nothing alarmed when results were overdue. Result: 2h20m lost and two fabricated status reports. Evidence: 07:04. *High.*
12. **Rollbacks that repeat version numbers overwrite artifacts.** checkpoint_03205.pt was rewritten by deploy 62's resume. Keep named copies of start points; quarantine covers files above the start, not the start itself. Evidence: 08:14. *High.*
13. **Own-vs-own self-play alone may starve defence.** Training eliminations were about 0.02–0.06. Losses against NK2 came late (day 28 flat; e5: main hero 0.64–0.70 of the strongest rival from day 35 at v3289; 15 towns lost empty). The causal link to the training regime is unproven (unclear). Evidence: 07:55; e5's memory 07:43–07:50. *Low.*
14. **An auxiliary head in a shared clipped optimizer group can starve the group's main heads** (grad norm 26.8 → 80.6 at weight 1). Zero-init its outputs and weight it down, or give it its own group. Evidence: 23:54. *Medium.*
