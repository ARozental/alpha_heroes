## 1. Summary

The period opens with a developer idea for the imitation loss: score NK2-imitation at round level, so the order of actions inside a turn doesn't matter. After two rounds of developer pushback, b1 built a "pending-set" teacher loss, plus a GradNorm-style closed-form controller for each loss term's share of the gradient (a8b587e, live 11-02 13:00). The developer added two rules on the way: a target counts only once it is visible, and policy and value must each get at least 10% of the gradient. The value term started at about 1/10,000 of the policy's gradient and took about 30 updates (×1.25 per update) to reach its 15% share. After that, the critic's explained variance roughly doubled. Campaign evals moved from about 0.33 to about 0.40, but the change is within the eval's noise.

From the evening of 11-02 through 11-03 noon, the work was almost all throughput, on the developer's "more speed for the same model".
- **What worked:** e5's TBB thread cap, more inference servers, a 4-window budget in the trainer's CUDA graphs, libz decoding into pinned buffers, and shifting game slots from the PC to the Mac. The biggest win came last: the forkserver preload had never applied in the real run. Each of 25 loader workers had imported its own torch (9.5 GB in all; 0.6 GB once fixed).
- **Result:** game production rose from 237 to about 357–400 games/h, and updates from about 9.5/h to 17–21/h.
- **Failures along the way:**
  - One crash caused by b1's own deploy (07:25 11-03; about 15 in-flight games lost).
  - Two wrong memory theories, both reverted.
  - A 25–35% speedup estimate that delivered 7%.
  - A GPU-memory leak found from a caught OOM.
  - bf16, ONNX Runtime, torch.compile and zstd were each measured and rejected.

From the evening of 11-03, the focus moved to why the policy plateaus against NK2.
- **Diagnosis:** self-play is peaceful (599 of 600 games end at the day cap). Near-empty heroes kept attacking neutral stacks a median 54× stronger: 224 attacks per 30 games, 97% lost.
- **Developer-directed changes, step by step:**
  - share weight ramped faster and scaled by map size;
  - an armyless hero worth about −1k in Φ;
  - exploration at 5 gold per tile;
  - fight features on MOVE. These were inert at lr 1e-5.
  - A feature audit the developer demanded found 5 dead MOVE features, a `behind` feature that had always been 0 (a bug), and a foresight head blind to suicides.
  - The rebuild: 4 strength estimates × 2, a mixer, meaningful foresight heads (ab52217), and an engine-response-only battle head (0b899a4). Then a learnable NK2-style "safety" prior (5805895). Suicides fell about 60%, from 275 to about 112 per 30 games.
- **Trades and questions:**
  - Trades: 135 per game, 77% with no purchase that day, about 213k Φ lost per game. The trade prior (50678d1) cut that only modestly.
  - Schema 11 (live 17:09 11-04): question guards, join/flee offers, garrison armies.
  - Two quick follow-up fixes:
    - a fake −760 hero "reward" that b1 itself had introduced;
    - a dwelling-YES prior, after finding we refused 467 of 481 dwelling recruit offers.
- **Where the period ends:** the 08c8106 deploy (visit_yes) is draining. Campaign evals are flat at about 0.42–0.47 on the new Φ, the battle eval is flat at +0.09, and value-loss metrics are still improving.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Imitation can be the bottleneck's mask.**
   - With the teacher pinned at 60% of the gradient, imitation metrics kept improving: agreement 0.38→0.45, teacher_ce 1.68→about 1.1. Self-play Φ also rose (244k→about 305k). The eval against NK2 stayed flat (about 0.40–0.45 over about 600 updates).
   - Lesson: track the target metric, not the auxiliary ones. High confidence (14:13 11-03, 15:25 11-04).
2. **Peaceful self-play doesn't train defence.**
   - 599 of 600 self-play games end at the cap, with 0.3% eliminations, against 31% eliminated vs NK2.
   - Share-based rewards in 3–4 player games make war a gift to bystanders. With an own-growth credit early in the game, attacking is close to pure cost.
   - A capped share reward needs opponent diversity (exploiters, past snapshots) for robustness. High confidence on the diagnosis (21:25–21:32 11-03); the fix was not tested here.
3. **Rare catastrophic actions aren't learned from outcome credit.**
   - A 1.5k-Φ hero loss is −0.1 to −0.2 SD of a 7-day credit, and only 38–50% of suicides come out negative. About 1% sampling per decision over hundreds of decisions yields about 9 suicides a game.
   - What fixed it: a domain prior with a learnable correction (−60%), not new features at lr 1e-5. High confidence (01:15, 12:31 11-04).
4. **Zero-init small layers at low LR are inert for days.**
   - Adam moves each weight by at most about lr per step: about 300 steps/h × 1e-5 = 0.003/h. Compute this before shipping a "the network will learn it" feature.
   - Sane initializations (hopeless × −3) are a stability-safe alternative to higher step sizes. High confidence (00:46–01:15, 08:40 11-04).
5. **Audit hand features for dead and saturating inputs before adding more.**
   - 5 of 14 MOVE features were dead and one (`behind`) was a bug. Win/loss/fight saturated, so a 3× and a 54× enemy looked alike.
   - Relative quantities (log-odds against this hero; value ÷ Φ) are what scale to large maps. High confidence (08:12 11-04).
6. **Aux heads need meaningful, dense labels.**
   - A foresight head labelled only on chosen tiles (about 1% hopeless) at weight 0.2 predicted P(battle) of 0.15–0.18 where a battle was near certain.
   - Labels that depend on our own policy are a confound. Predict the engine's response. High confidence (08:12, 09:45 11-04).
7. **Under WSL on a budget PC, RAM, not CPU or GPU, is the currency.**
   - Every game in flight cost a PC loader worker, about 0.41 GB before the fix. The single biggest throughput gain was a startup-order bug that had never applied the forkserver preload: 9.5 GB → 0.6 GB.
   - Verify a memory fix in the real process tree (the forkserver command line), not in an isolated test. High confidence (11:42 11-03).
8. **Estimate learner speedups from GPU busy time, not CPU-side clocks.**
   - A 25–35% estimate became 7%; the "61→36 ms step" headline became 57→53 ms for the full micro-batch.
   - The bottleneck flipped between games and learner about four times in 24 h. Re-measure after every change. High confidence.
9. **CPU-inference runtime tricks don't help a batch-1 forward of about 20k tiny ops.**
   - bf16 0.95×, ONNX 1.06×, torch.compile 1.05×. Only real compute (about 6.7 ms of about 17 ms) is accelerable.
   - torch.compile (Dynamo) traced `tensor == IntEnum` as a scalar comparison, which is silently wrong where shapes broadcast. High confidence (06:58, 07:41 11-03).
10. **Check engine facts in source before asserting them.** Wrong claims about pathfinding through guards, NK2's dwelling answer and empty-hire Φ were each corrected only after the developer challenged them or data contradicted them. The developer's game knowledge ("A player with the UI can't") beat b1's inference. High confidence.
11. **Eval noise.**
    - About 12 of 48 games flip elimination status between any two consecutive evals, and the median per-game change is 0. Single-eval moves of ±0.05–0.09 (1–1.3σ) are not signal.
    - b1 over-read rises ("plateau is breaking") but correctly read the v832 dip as noise. Later memory (11-12) puts run-to-run noise at about ±0.11, larger than the reported SE. High confidence.
12. **Rare-path diagnostics can kill a long run.** Code that runs every N updates (loss_noise) must be tested on the production path and wrapped so it can't end the run. High confidence (07:25 11-03).
13. **Learned biases can lock in when an option is rarely sampled.** A NO bias of 4–6 logits learned before YES options were distinguishable can't be undone by PPO, because YES is never sampled. Priors on the starved option fixed it (3%→42%). Medium-high confidence (17:41–18:08 11-04).
14. **AI-assisted development.**
    - An approval wait on a rule-conflicting fix can stall progress for hours when a rule-compliant alternative exists. Surface all options at once.
    - Contradictory LR advice within 48 h (raise, then lower), both unexecuted, suggests rule-following without a model of the run. Medium confidence.
