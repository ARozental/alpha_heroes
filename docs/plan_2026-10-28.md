### Round 6: one horizon for both policies, and a guard against quick gains (2026-10-28, late)

- **Battle policy maximizes the final V alone** (`policy_heads` (0, 0, 0, 1)). V stays unchained: chained to the round
  heads (2026-10-23) it inherited their material swings and taught the defence passivity. The round heads V0 (end of
  this round), V1, V2 stay chained to each other (detached) as auxiliary targets.
- **Campaign heads chained exactly as the battle's round heads**: V5 = V2 (detached) + its correction, V7 = V5
  (detached) + its correction, all starting at 0; each head's loss reaches only its own weights (tested).
- **No day+1 head, no extra battle head** (end of round already exists, V0). Shares barely move in a day, and a
  shorter root makes the chain's starting view more myopic.
- **Credit check** (`labels.credit_evidence`, `credit_gaps`, `credit_trust`): through the chain V7 starts as V2's
  view and inherits V2's explained variance, so the trust gauge alone would hand the credit to a myopic V7. The step's
  error (its estimate of the day-7 share minus the share then) after each action type, against the same player's
  other decisions; one sample per game-player; evidence kept over ~10 updates. A gap over 3 standard errors (a quick
  gain that costs by day 7, or a slow gain the heads miss) cuts the step's estimate, to nothing at 6: the credit goes
  to the real outcome, never to the 2-day shortcut. Simulated: honest heads |t| <= 2.4 over 30 updates; a 0.01-share
  overpricing of one type t ~ 15 in 3 updates. Logged as campaign/credit_trust and campaign_credit/<type>.
  The first version's two bugs (found before any run): decisions counted as samples (they share the week's luck:
  honest heads flagged in 3-8 of 10 updates), and a failed check raised the 2-day shortcut's weight.
- **Heads, the rule** (the user asked about d+1, end of round, end of combat/campaign): a head is worth adding only if
  the policy's credit never reads it (not chained into V7, not a bootstrap) or it is the objective, and its label
  holds signal the others lack. Battle: end of this round is V0, end of combat is V (the objective); nothing to add.
  Campaign: d+1 no (inside d+2; as the chain's root, V7 would start from a 1-day view); end of game no (the 56-day cap
  makes it a horizon that shrinks to the same day on day 56; too noisy before).
- **Shared luck (the user's question):** decisions' labels share luck (same day: the same d+7 label; k days apart:
  (H - k)/H of it; 2 players: opposite). It biases no fit; it breaks statistics that count samples (the check's bug)
  and costs efficiency. The textbook fix (GLS = whiten; for overlapping horizons, bootstrap a step at a time: TD,
  fixed-horizon TD) measured in a linear toy, same data, data-efficiency over raw d+7 labels by the edge's half-life
  (<1 day, 1, 3, 7, 23 days): one-day ladder V1..V7 6.1x, 3.5x, 1.8x, 1.4x, 1.1x; composing V2/V5/V7 (V7 from V5 two
  days on, V5 from V2 three days on) 3.4x, 2.8x, 1.7x, 1.4x, 1.1x. Built (round 7, below). No d+1 head: the ladder's extra
  over composing is in edges gone within a day.
- **Fixed: the cap.** Past the day cap there is no label (nan): left out of the value loss and the policy's credit.

### Round 7: the new value loss (2026-10-28, late; the user's go)

  Weight 0.5 each (`growth_weight`).

- **Masks:** no label past a day cap the game did not end at (`labels.tables`: a final snapshot after the last daily

### Round 12: the end of the game (2026-10-28, night; the user: "do you need an end of game head for V?")

- **The problem (the user's):** the policy aims at the share 7 days on, so whatever pays later is invisible: an
  Estates' gold beats Offense or Earth Magic, and Wisdom (the way to level 3+ spells weeks on) is never worth taking.
  A hand-made bias would fix the cases we know and none of the others. Overrides Round 6's "no end-of-game head".
- **Random ends:** each training game's cap is day 28 + a geometric number of days (mean 28, at most 112;
  `learner.draw_days`). Past day 28 every day is as likely to be the last: the model can't see the end coming, so
  nothing is worth grabbing just before it. The AI never sees the cap; evaluations stay at 56 days. `game_seconds`
  360 -> 600 for the longer games.
- **V_end** (`model.end_head`, zero-init, starts at 1 / players; replaces the 14-day helper): the share at the game's
  end (`labels.end_labels`: one player left, or the cap; a knocked-out player 0; nan at the time limit). Graded
  against itself at the player's next day start, mixed with the real end by the trust in it (`end_targets`, as the
  ladder). Trained at a share head's weight.
- **Credit:** advantage = (1 - b) x the 7-day credit + b x the end credit (`labels.end_advantages`, `blend_credit`):
  V_end at the player's next turn decision minus here (td), 25% of it V_end a week on (corrects td's blind spots
  without the whole game's luck), V_end from the model as the update starts (the KL anchor's pass). b =
  `end_credit_max` (1) x the trust in V_end: its explained variance at the day starts *over the share prior's*
  (plain EV would trust a V_end that knows only the player count), smoothed, / 0.4. So the switch to the end
  happens by itself when V_end has learned something; `end_credit_max=0` keeps V_end for testing only.
  Decisions within a week of a cap had no credit (no share 7 days on); they get the end's.
- No credit check on the end: one game's decisions share one end, so a check relative to the player's other
  decisions measures nothing.

## Part 1. The battle model: why it got worse, and the fix

### What happened (measured)

| | 2106 (start) | 2840 (best balanced) | 3139 (drifted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| p(SHOOT) where legal: median / share below 0.5 | 1.00 / 22% | 0.99 / 12% | **0.32 / 55%** |
| decision entropy: median / 90th percentile | 1.40 / 3.2 | 1.64 / 3.2 | **2.05 / 4.0** |
| eval "shooter idle" (BattleAI / MMAI) | 0.05 / 0.09 | 0.04 / 0.06 | **0.39 / 0.51** |

The policy flattened toward uniform: a median p(SHOOT) of 0.32 is about uniform over a shooter's 3-4 types.

### Why (measured, two independent datasets)

| loss term (as trained) | coherence of 20 minibatch gradients (pure noise: 0.22) | effect on log p(SHOOT), t-statistic |
|---|---|---|
| policy: generated / campaign / joint | 0.20 / 0.33 / 0.18 | +0.3 / -0.7 / -0.05 |
| **entropy bonus** (0.01): generated / campaign / joint | **0.96 / 0.98 / 0.96** | **-27.5 / -9.3 / -17.9** |
| value loss (0.5, shared trunk) | 0.94 / 0.92 / 0.87 | -5.3 / -4.4 / -6.4 |
| advantage parts alone: window on V / round-2 score | 0.26 / 0.23 | -0.4 / -0.95 |

- The **policy gradient is indistinguishable from noise** even averaged over 5,000 decisions, in every source and
  every part of the advantage. Its effect on shooting has |t| < 1.
- The **entropy bonus is consistent** (the same direction every minibatch) and lowers p(SHOOT): the bonus maximises
  entropy, which spreads a sharp choice's probability over the others. The value loss pushes the same way, ~10x
  weaker, through the shared trunk.
- A noise term grows like sqrt(steps); a consistent term grows like steps. With these magnitudes the consistent term
  overtakes after a few hundred updates: the drift began ~update 2600, 500 updates in.
- v15-window had the same bonus and did not drift: its policy signal was ~2x stronger. In joint1 the campaign battles
  (40% of decisions, their own direction unrelated to the generated one: cosine -0.05 to -0.08) diluted it, and under
  the joint per-side normalisation they got **50% larger advantages** (mean |A| 0.94 against 0.62).

**Your hypothesis, checked.** Lower LR, bigger batches, Adam's longer memory (betas: beta1 = 0.9 averages ~10
minibatches, beta2 = 0.999 ~1,000) and Muon all reduce *noise*. The drift is not noise: it is a consistent push winning
over a weak consistent signal, and with Adam in this noise-dominated regime (its sqrt(v) is set by the noise) the
signal and the push shrink by the same factor whatever the learning rate or batch size, so their ratio, which sets
the drift, does not change. They would slow the drift and the learning together. I recommend against them as the
fix; they stay worth having for jitter (B4).

### Changes

**B1. The entropy bonus becomes a floor (the fix).** Replace `- 0.01 * mean(H)` by
`+ c * mean(max(0, H_floor - H))` with H_floor = 0.3 nats and c = 0.1: only decisions sharper than the floor are
pushed back; every other decision feels nothing. 0.3 is about the 10th percentile of today's per-decision entropy
(5-12% of decisions are below it). Same change for the campaign PPO, which has the same constant bonus.
- predicted: the consistent push (|t| 9-28) disappears for 88-95% of decisions. The drift stops: p(SHOOT) where legal
  stays at a median of >= 0.95 and the eval's shooter-idle at <= 0.08. Entropy stops rising and slowly falls as the
  weak signal accumulates.
- risk: less exploration. PPO's clip (20%) still bounds how fast a choice sharpens, and the floor stops collapse.

**B2. Normalise advantages per (side, source).** Generated and campaign battles become different groups, as attack
and defence already are (losses.normalize_advantages documents the same failure between the sides).
- predicted: the campaign share of each step's normalised signal falls from ~50% to its decision share (40%).

**B3. Evals and the deployed model use an EMA of the weights** (decay 0.99 per update, a ~100-update window).
Training is unchanged.
- predicted: a large part of the eval-to-eval swings comes from where the noise has just moved the weights
  (shooter-idle went 0.54 -> 0.06 -> 0.51 within 400 updates). Averaging the last ~100 updates removes most of it,
  so eval trends become readable. The EMA lags the trained weights by ~100 updates (~40 min).

**B4. Not now: lower LR / bigger batches / beta1 0.95 / Muon** (see above). Revisit if, after B1-B3, evals still
jitter.

**B5. Smaller battle records in memory.** A decision's record is 0.4 MB (mostly the spell-by-hex tables). The 16k-
decision buffer held ~6 GB: the trainer's 19 GB footprint, the swapping, and the 40% system time.
- plan: keep only what training reads, with the spell-by-hex tables sparse (most entries are 0).
- predicted: about -5 GB.

**B6. Restart from checkpoint 2840** (+0.076, every eval set positive), not from scratch: the weights are fine; the
training rule was not.

Later, not part of this plan: the policy signal itself is very weak (noise-level at 5k decisions), so the battle model
learns slowly whatever we do. The real lever is a lower-variance target, e.g. search (Gumbel, already in C++).
Search lost to PPO in October (16 sims, an early model with a weak V), so it waits for a better value head.

## Part 2. Campaign credit: no decay, and the measured answer on a window

There is no exponential decay anywhere now:
- the battle model uses your linear round window (lambda only belongs to the unused "gae" option);
- the campaign's advantage is pure Monte Carlo over fixed days: the flat mean of the shares at d+2, d+5 and d+7, minus
  the price, minus what the value heads predicted;
- the reward-against-distance discount (turns^1.3) is dropped, as you said.

The question was whether the campaign should take the battle model's window form: each decision's credit being the
value head's change until the player's next decision that day.
- predicted (first principles): ~7x less variance, so much higher SNR.
- **measured: the opposite.** On moves onto certain gains (resource piles, chests, campfires) against moves onto empty
  tiles:

| credit | gain moves - empty moves | std of all | SNR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monte Carlo (as trained) | +0.0114 | 0.048 | **+0.23** (t ~ +3) |
| window / TD | -0.0014 | 0.0072 | **-0.20** (t ~ -2.5) |

- **Why:** every decision of a day has the same label, so the value head is trained to give the states before and
  after an action that day the same value. It is blind within the day; its differences there are dominated by other
  things (e.g. movement points spent).
- **Decision:** keep Monte Carlo. Re-measure after C1-C3: the new features give the value head what it needs to see a
  pickup.

## Part 3. Campaign model: features as ops (the graph changes)

The pattern is the battle model's (`layers.ActionScore`): each choice's logit gets its features' score next to the
learned score. Two differences, as you asked:
- the per-feature weights start at **defaults** that make the feature steer from the first game (the battle model
  starts them at 0);
- the tiny and wide layers start at 0 and learn the corrections.

All features are bounded and scale-free, in the battle model's style:
- fractions;
- `x / (x + c)` at several scales;
- log-ratios through a sigmoid;
- never `x / 50`.

### C0. Where it plugs in

Today a MOVE logit is `move_out(gelu(cells * (1 + gamma_h) + beta_h))`: the windows' convolution features, FiLM'd by
the hero token. New:

    move_logit[b, h, r, c] = move_out(...)                                     (learned, as now)
                           + sum_k w_k * f_k[b, h, r, c]                       (w_k start at the defaults below)
                           + tiny(f)[b, h, r, c]                               (8 hidden, output starts at 0)
                           + wide(f, hero token, mean and max of f over the legal tiles)[b, h, r, c]   (32 hidden, output 0)

- f is computed only for the windows of present heroes, like the convolutions, so the cost is K x 33 x 33 x ~6 numbers.
- f_value and f_loss also join the tiles' input features (cell_in), so the convolution can see value beyond today's
  reach and learn to head toward it.

### C1. What visiting an object is worth (f_value)

    gate[tile]  = visitable tile (TILE_VISITABLE: only the object's entrance, not the tiles it blocks)
                  and the object is not a hero (34) or a wandering monster (54)
                  and not ours (w_owner != OWNER_SELF)
                  and not visited (neither TILE_VISITED_BY_PLAYER nor TILE_VISITED_BY_HERO: weekly and per-hero objects)
    f_value     = gate * (1 + t_type[object] + t_sub[bucket(object, subtype)])

- t_type is an embedding of 256 object types x 1. t_sub is one of the 1,021 (object, subtype) buckets x 1, so a gold
  mine and a sawmill, a level-1 and a level-7 dwelling, gold and wood can differ. Both start at 0, so every object
  starts at the same worth, 1, as you asked.
- w_value starts at **+4**. With ~300 legal tiles and ~5 objects in today's reach, a +4 makes about half of all moves
  go to an object (5e^4 / (295 + 5e^4) ~ 0.48). **Measured now: 13%** of moves go to a sure gain, 69% to an empty
  tile.
- Enemy and neutral towns are objects too, so a capture can learn a large worth. Their guards come in through f_loss.
- predicted:
  - moves onto objects 13% -> ~45% at the start of training;
  - resources gathered per hero-day up ~2-3x;
  - faster early growth: the day-28 score against Nullkiller2 above 0.5 sooner than in joint1 (where it took 80
    versions to reach ~0.45).

### C2. Expected army loss (f_loss)

    ratio  = log(our hero's strength) - log(theirs)          (already computed per tile: monsters, enemy heroes and towns)
    f_loss = has_enemy * min(1, exp(-2 * ratio))             (Nullkiller2's loss / army = (their strength / ours)^2, capped at all)
    guarded tiles: the 3x3 max-pool of the monster tiles' f_loss over tiles flagged TILE_GUARDED
                   (stepping next to a monster starts its fight)

- w_loss starts at **-4**: a fight that costs our whole army is e^-4 (~2%) as likely as the same tile without it; a
  10%-loss fight is barely touched (-0.4).
- predicted: campaign heroes retreat in ~60% of their fights now (finding 14); expected ~20-25%. That also stops the
  ~1,280 decisions an update of mostly pointless campaign battles from flooding the battle learner.

### C3. Danger and defence (f_danger, f_defend, town danger)

    reach_e(tile)  = sigmoid(4 * (1 - dist(e, tile) / R))      Chebyshev tiles, same level; R = softplus(rho), starting at 15 (a day's walk)
    threat_e(h)    = sigmoid(2 * (log S_e - log S_h))          enemy hero e beats our hero h
    f_danger       = max over visible enemy heroes e of reach_e * threat_e                      [K, 33, 33, 16] -> max
    town_danger(t) = max_e reach_e(t) * sigmoid(2 * (log S_e - log S_garrison+hero(t)))
    f_defend       = [the tile is our town t] * town_danger(t)

- Default weights: w_danger **-2**, w_defend **+2**. Town danger also goes into the town token and adds to that
  town's RECRUIT logits with a default of +1.
- (Built otherwise: R is the constant `features.DANGER_RANGE` (15), not learned; the danger enters a tile's value in
  gold with a learnable gain (`danger_gain`), round 4's "Danger".)
- This is the matrix you asked about. Battle: our units x their targets. Here: our heroes x tiles (MOVE), enemy
  heroes x our tiles and towns (danger), our heroes x enemy heroes (who beats whom; also into the hero tokens, like
  the battle model's exposure features).
- predicted: little effect in self-play, where no one attacks (finding 13); it matters with C7.

### C4. Representation fixes (the "def / 50" kind)

| now | change to |
|---|---|
| `h_primary / 10` | `scales(x, (2, 8, 32))` per skill, plus log(1 + 0.05 attack) and log(1 + 0.05 defense) (the fighting-strength factors) |
| `h_level / 10`, `log exp / 10` | `scales(level, (2, 8, 32))`, plus the fraction of the way to the next level |
| `h_move / 2000`, `w_move_left / 2000` | move / move_max (the fraction of the day left), plus move_max / 1500 once |
| `log mana / 5` | mana / mana_max, plus `scales(mana, (5, 25, 125))` |

- predicted: small but free; these are the inputs the danger and loss features lean on.

### C5. The type head sees the moves' worth

- type_logit[MOVE] += w_best * (the best default move score over the legal tiles), with w_best starting at 0.5.
  This is the battle model's "value of the best follow-up" in the type logits.
- predicted: fewer turns ended while an object is in reach. Today END is ~33% of turn decisions.

## Part 4. Memory (phase 2)

What the engine already keeps (the inputs have it):
- revealed tiles stay revealed with their objects;
- visited flags per player and per hero.

What is missing, in order of importance:
1. **The map beyond 16 tiles.** On small maps the whole map is 2 x 36 x 36 tiles, about two windows. Channels:
   - object, subtype bucket, owner, visited flags;
   - monster creature and its count range;
   - each tile's f_value with the shared t tables (one value function for the window and the map).

   A 2-layer convolution; each own hero's token reads it through 8 sectors around the hero (distance-weighted
   sums), the global token through a pooled summary.
   - predicted: fewer moves to empty tiles; heroes head for value clusters out of sight.
2. **An event log** as tokens, like the battle model's e_*: the last 16 of:
   - battles (where, against what, result, value lost);
   - towns taken or lost;
   - heroes hired or lost;
   - enemy heroes seen within a day of our towns.
3. **Enemy memory.** An enemy hero no longer visible keeps its token with "days since seen" and the last army seen
   in detail (in battle or adjacent). It is kept in AlphaMapAI as raw observations, the way v15 keeps seen spells.
4. **Intent.** Each hero's last destination and how many days ago it was set: a flag on that tile and a feature in
   the hero token, so multi-day trips do not dither.

All of these are remembered observations, not a learned recurrent state: cheaper with PPO, and you can inspect them.
