## 1. Summary

The period opened on a single fixed scenario, castle_vs_inferno, with a v1 transformer, PPO self-play, and a just-built Gumbel-AlphaZero search. The developer started a line-by-line review of the model ("what is x here?"). The developer then drove a redesign around their principle of "op engineering and not feature engineering":
- exact, engine-checked game ops (damage, kills, reach, threat);
- features that are bounded, scale-free and built only from the current battle, each landing on its own action's logit;
- feature weights learned from zero.

The AI went through several stages:
- a proof-of-concept rule model (attack the most valuable sure kill: 103/103 in VCMI);
- a zero-parameter ONNX port of VCMI's StupidAI (865/865 decisions given starting stack sizes), which exposed engine facts (hate, `getTotalHealth`, Blind);
- model v2 (bounded features, ActionScore heads, 20 unit slots);
- creature spells (contract v9).

Search-trained play then failed badly against PPO (attack −0.90 vs −0.03 relative to BattleAI). The developer demanded an explanation that "would have let you predict in advance how badly it is going to fail".

On 10-24 the developer set a loop: 400-epoch rounds to beat `20261023-1902-up-ppo` (total_score +0.074). The rounds produced:
- v3: signed half-tokens (their idea), RMSNorm/SwiGLU, Lanchester strength prior;
- a zero-sum asset reward (their question; the AI's recommendation; the developer said "build now");
- total_score as the single metric (their demand);
- random seeds, linear LR, and an MPS memory-leak fix;
- v11/v12: engine-played attack values, ability bag, multi-scale features, hero value, an escape prior.

v11 regressed because of a `score_now` input, which the developer's "why is it learning slower?" surfaced. The castle score rose only modestly: +0.118 for R5, +0.135 for v12 at 1000 epochs. The AI conceded that the new features gave "no gain" on this one matchup.

That evening the developer had the AI build the random battle generator from their `generator.toml` spec. Playing generated battles exposed bugs hidden in every experiment so far:
- sieges were silently played by StupidAI;
- VCMI gave a random starting-bonus artifact in about a third of games;
- tome spells were invisible to the model;
- neutral battles ended before they started.

Overnight (10-24 22:30 → 10-25 07:00) `gen-v13` trained 4,000 epochs on generated battles, with three engine/AI hangs fixed in flight. It ended at +0.089 on its 20-battle eval and +0.098 on 40 fresh battles; the castle-only model scored −0.048 there.

On 10-25 the developer asked for a full design for a run that beats every battle AI with no exploitable failure modes. The AI designed and built contract v14:
- 14 units per side; tactics and war machines played by the model; no StupidAI;
- exposure features, a rule-artifact bag, example weights;
- actors feeding a staggered buffer from real Nullkiller2 games plus generated battles;
- eval vs BattleAI and MMAI.

The period ends mid-smoke-run (`20261025-1656-smoke-long`): healthy, slowly improving (−0.031 → −0.017 vs BattleAI by update 200), learner-bound. The developer had just objected that the AI had put MMAI/BattleAI into training ("training is our model against itself").

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Search under dice with a tiny budget gives a noise target.** Gumbel/mctx defaults assume deterministic games. With 16 simulations over 16 considered actions, each action got one dice roll plus one value-head guess. Min-max rescaling × 5.1 nats then made the target "copy the luckiest roll". Check before spending compute: (a) the agreement of the chosen action across re-rolled dice; (b) the fraction of simulations reaching terminals against value-head quality. Evidence: 10-23 22:46, 10-24 10:15. Confidence: high for "the target was noisy"; the mechanism was not directly measured.
2. **Never give the model the current score.** The value heads latch onto it, and any action that delays loss (fleeing) looks good. Evidence: the v11 ablation (defence kills 21% → 73% when removed), 10-24 17:26. Confidence: high.
3. **Fixed seeds overfit even in self-play.** On 2 fixed battles, shooters learned to stop shooting and the run collapsed. Random battle seeds fixed it. Evidence: up-zs-lin, 10-24 14:44. Confidence: medium-high.
4. **Asymmetric scenarios masquerade as seat problems.** "Defence is weak" was really "Inferno is weaker". Measure against a reference AI on the same seed and seat, or mirror/randomise armies. Evidence: 10-24 08:14. Confidence: high.
5. **A cheap analytic value prior (Lanchester: health × damage per round) is strong and fixes credit sign.** It explained 72% of final-reward variance untrained. It turned the wrong-sign kill credit (−0.06) positive (+0.24/+0.36) and gave the best round-2 run. Evidence: 10-24 10:15, 11:34. Confidence: medium-high. The total_score gain is confounded by seed choice.
6. **Engine-as-oracle checks beat self-written tests.** Matching VCMI's StupidAI decision by decision exposed hate, starting-size health caps and Blind's retaliation effect. Engine masks validated reach. Parity tests caught reward drift. Evidence: 10-23 16:11, 18:29; 10-24 12:04. Confidence: high.
7. **Distribution shift is the best bug-finder.** The first hour of generated battles found four latent bugs that the single scenario had hidden for days, including a hidden random artifact in about ⅓ of all prior games (eval noise in every earlier comparison). Evidence: 10-24 20:27–21:37. Confidence: high. Lesson: build the varied generator before many rounds of architecture tuning on one matchup.
8. **Architecture tuning on one fixed matchup is nearly uninformative.** Three model versions (v3, v11, v12) gave ≈0 net gain on castle_vs_inferno. The gains came from reward (zero-sum), seeds, LR schedule and longer training (+0.074 → +0.135). The generator-trained model then generalised: +0.098 on fresh battles vs −0.048 for the castle model. Evidence: 10-24 18:23, 10-25 07:03. Confidence: medium.
9. **Small eval sets cannot show per-eval progress.** With 20 battles, one flipped battle moves total_score by 0.03–0.05. A fixed-set eval plateaued while 40 fresh battles showed steady improvement. The same castle model scored −0.126, −0.081 or −0.048 depending on the generated set. Evidence: 10-25 03:47, 05:48, 07:03. Confidence: high. This recurs much later (eval-slide memory, 11-11).
10. **Self-play plus a reward where heroes are worth as much as armies produces retreat equilibria (≈40% of battles), costly against a never-retreating opponent.** The developer's reframe was more useful than the AI's "it's reward-consistent": give the model an input for whether it can actually be finished before its next turn. Evidence: 10-25 00:02, 08:14. Confidence: medium.
11. **On Apple MPS, variable batch shapes leak memory** (a compiled graph per shape, ~100 MB each). Fix shapes. The AI first blamed other apps. Evidence: 10-24 13:28. Confidence: high.
12. **Battle-only engine modes hide dialog and turn assumptions** (level-ups, start flags, no round limit). Budget for a stall watchdog plus saved-scenario reproduction and resume. Evidence: three hangs, 10-25. Confidence: high.
13. **Data efficiency:** discarding most decisions per expensive battle (2 of ~50) is a 25× waste. Real full games are a cheap source (~2 battle records/s) but dominated by trivial neutral fights (74 of 98), so they need weighting or stratification. Evidence: 10-23 19:18, 10-25 raw 21:54Z/22:01Z. Confidence: medium.
14. **AI-assisted process:**
    - The developer repeatedly lost track of what was running ("what are you testing now?" ×2; "I'm not sure what you are testing now").
    - The AI drifted from "find bugs/architecture issues" into hyperparameter or opponent experiments.
    - It added unexplained parameters and opponents (MMAI) and deferred clear calls.
    - Cheap correctives that worked: a single metric, stated test purpose per run, explanations that must predict.

    Evidence: §5. Confidence: high.

## 9. Numbers worth citing

- **Hardware:** MacBook, 14 cores, 24 GB RAM (swap reached 16.2/17.4 GB before the leak fix), ~202 GB free disk. All training on Apple MPS.
- **Model sizes:**
  - v1: 0.9 ms C++ inference, 45–52 ms/step.
  - v2: 264k–284k params, 4.6–5.5 ms inference, 60 ms/step plus 86 ms facts per 64 decisions per epoch.
  - v3: first 511k, then 317k; 9.5 ms inference, 100 ms/step.
  - v11: 286.6k. v12: 288.8k.
- **Engine-played attack values:** ~5 ms/decision, no epoch slowdown (8.3 s vs 8.7–9.3 s/epoch). Network ~16 ms/decision in C++ (10-24 16:21).
- **Per-battle overhead:** 1.5–2 s (map 0.25 s, start 0.5–1 s, launch ~1 s) vs 3.3 s battle alone or 7.5–8.5 s with 8 parallel. `.vmap` = 1.8 KB.
- **Throughput:** castle runs ~7–13 s/epoch (16 battles); search run 31–36 s/epoch; gen-v13 7.4 s/epoch (4.3 s GPU, 2.6 s waiting), 4,000 epochs ≈ 8.2 h.
- **Data volumes:** a castle run uses ~10–12 MB of disk (0.04–0.16 MB/min); generated battles 0.1–1 MB each (0.5–9 MB/min); gen-v13 run folder 80 MB.
- **v14 actor smoke run:**
  - learner ~8.6 s per 400-decision update (~280 decision-passes/s);
  - buffer 3,000 decisions ≈ 1 GB (~0.3 MB/decision);
  - learner memory 2.8–3.3 GB, 5.7 GB peak in evals;
  - full eval 208 battles in 116–183 s vs BattleAI, 260 s vs MMAI (~7.5 min total);
  - 51% real data from ~10 games/update; record age ~1.1 versions;
