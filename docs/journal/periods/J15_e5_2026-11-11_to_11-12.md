## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **A single 48-game eval arm cannot carry a decision.** The same weights scored 0.861, then 0.697 (paired −0.164 ± 0.080). v3301 scored 0.634, then 0.775. One PC eval swings about ±0.11. Four regime flips in 12 hours came from treating single arms as signal. For Heroes-like games with high outcome variance, budget at least 2 replicates per arm, run alone, before touching the live run. Confidence: high (07:58, 08:49).
2. **Evals sharing a machine with other work read low.** v3255 scored 0.685 at load about 11–14 against 0.805 alone (+0.121 ± 0.086). On a budget rig where evals, a training worker and harness runs share CPUs, compare only arms run under equal load. Confidence: medium (1.4 se; mechanism unclear, possibly time caps or inference latency).
3. **Optimizer-state proxies mislead right after a checkpoint or regime change.** Adam moments carry the old regime. The signal fraction fell 0.126 → 0.032 in 3 updates after a rollback, and ratio_body went 0.28 → 0.13 over about 6 updates. A rollback based on these readings was wrong. Judge by measured play, replicated. Confidence: high (00:05, 00:16, 08:13).
4. **Pure self-play between equal copies produces uneventful games.** Training eliminations fell 0.10 → 0.02, and the trunk signal was about 1/4 of the mixed regime's. Defence is never exercised, while the eval opponent attacks. Mixing in stronger-playing games (NK2 at 0.9 per move) gave a cleaner gradient. Whether it gave faster learning is unresolved. Confidence: medium (23:16, 08:13).
5. **Noise-based credit horizons work, but the shrinkage statistic needs precision weighting.** The 2-day credit cut variance to about 1/3 on real rows. The between-type F rode on one thin type (ARMY with 8 game-players). Confidence: medium (18:22, 19:23, 08:38).
6. **Cheap counterfactual decompositions beat games for design triage.** Swapping inputs live versus blind per consumer and scoring R = 1 − KL(live‖mode)/KL(live‖blind) on about 1,000 recorded decisions took minutes. It killed the late-only design and the routing hypothesis. Caveat: R measures closeness to the reference policy, not play quality. A design with R≈0 played within noise of live. Confidence: high for triage (02:14–03:49, 03:57).
7. **Imitating a scripted planner:** its candidate set is locally predictable (AUC 0.96–0.98), but its pick is not (top-1 about 0.30; precision 0.75 at recall 0.18). The policy's KL dependence on the planner was large, but the play value was small (−0.03 to −0.13). Strong policy dependence on an expert input does not mean the input is valuable. The longer training ran with a live input, the deeper the dependence grew (TRAVEL routes shifted in 26 updates). Confidence: medium (02:26, 03:56, 04:43, 03:31).
8. **CUDA-graph training has non-obvious failure modes:** any host read (`float()`) inside a captured backward breaks capture; a graph object held as a local re-records every update; a first-update time gate includes the capture cost. Test on the GPU box, gate on steady state. A CPU dev machine (the Mac) cannot reveal any of these. Confidence: high (19:32, 01:08, 01:15).
9. **Background waits must use PIDs or done-files, never `pgrep -f <pattern>`** when the pattern appears in the launching command. One self-match cost 2h20m of overnight evidence and produced a false load report. Also check within one interval that the queued job started. Confidence: high (07:05).
10. **In multi-agent setups, "started" is not "done".** A peer's deploy failure went unseen for 90 minutes until the human asked a one-line status question. A 5-minute outcome rule plus self-verification fixed it. Confidence: high (21:04–21:06).
11. **A state-fingerprint loop guard is a cheap waste detector.** It surfaced about 9 wasted exchange decisions per game and a doubling under drift. The developer's preferred answer to waste is a tiny action price, not masks or negative credit. Confidence: medium (19:05, 22:04, 22:46).
12. **Untrained-from-priors baselines are informative for H3-like games.** Priors alone scored 0.123 against 0.51 trained. A broken start (no recruiting, troop shuffling) showed up at the action-type level, so a fresh start would have needed recalibrated priors. Confidence: medium (18:26).
13. **When a developer offers an alternative reading of noisy data, take it seriously.** The developer's 08:07 suspicion (mixed games produced the gain; all-own gain was optimizer memory) fit the logs better than e5's midnight conclusion. Confidence: medium.
