## 1. Summary

The period opens with the developer asking for a definition of "the value of an empire" (Φ). The goal was to train a campaign (adventure-map) AI on the objective "my Φ / all players' Φ after N days", with N ≤ 7, and to avoid biased failure modes. The AI proposed a price-based Φ with extras (army premium, command factor, accrued growth, threat discount). The developer rejected most of the extras on 10-27 14:00 with detailed corrections, then said "build it now". In about an hour the AI built:
- a C++ adventure AI (AlphaMapAI);
- the `campaign_model` package;
- a shared value table (`game_values`);
- a random-map tool;
- PPO on d+2/d+6/d+8 share labels.

By 23:27 it had added joint training: the campaign model learns from scratch while the existing battle model continues, plays every campaign battle, and learns from those battles too. There was also an eval against Nullkiller2 (NK2), which needed a 5-line engine change. The 9-hour run **joint1** (10-27 23:40 → 10-28 ~09:10) found 16 issues. The campaign learned the economy (self-play Φ at day 56 went 117k → 249k; day-28 score vs NK2 0.29 → ~0.45) but never learned to survive: it was knocked out in ~70% of eval games, while only 2 of 1,223 self-play players were ever knocked out. The battle model drifted into idle shooters.

On 10-28 the developer pushed back hard ("this sounds wrong!", "I think it messes up a LOT"). That produced:
- a measured diagnosis of the battle drift: the entropy bonus beating a noise-level policy gradient;
- `docs/plan_2026-10-28.md`;
- a day of low-power redesign: campaign contracts v3→v6, Nullkiller2's logic ported as bounded learnable priors and features, a whole-map CNN, a TRAVEL action, value audits and invariant tests, composed ("ladder") value targets, growth heads, and an end-of-game head V_end with random game lengths;
- war machines at half price.

**joint2** started 10-28 23:22. The night brought several crashes and fixes, the battle model frozen at 3078 after a second shooter drift, and commits 5604d8e/3239517. On 10-29 the drift was traced to the AI's own entropy-floor fix, and the weight average (EMA) was shown to be worse than the live weights. Additive battle "value priors" and deterministic BattleAI evals were added (dd8c6b8). A battle-only retrain (battle-priors) failed: +0.079 → +0.071.

The developer then set battle LR to 3e-5 and shifted the battle training mix toward campaign battles. The AI found that endless battles had been killing 30–60% of campaign games and fixed it (0b4d641). The period ends with joint2 running both models:
- battle eval rising: +0.074 → +0.079 → +0.082 at epoch 4167, idle shooters 0.022 vs BattleAI;
- campaign at 0.227 ± 0.085 vs NK2 (paired +0.103 ± 0.063 over its first eval);
- still eliminated in 17 of 24 games, with 0.17 heroes at day 56 against NK2's 3.67.

## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **League opponents passive after every resume [fd6b].**
   - Cause: snapshots registered as `v00050`, requested as `v50`.
   - Found by the adversarial review; undetected across 5+ resumes of v15-window.
   - Impact: 12.6% of recent battles were against a do-nothing opponent, inflating win rates.
   - Fixed in 3263898. Category: infra.
2. **KL early stop against a stale policy [fd6b].**
   - 40 updates stopped after a single step, and the learning rate cut in a sawtooth.
   - Fixed in 3263898.
   - A variant appeared later: one noisy minibatch could trigger the stop (15% of v15 updates stopped early, 99% with mean KL under half the line). Fixed 10-28 16:50 with a 4-minibatch mean.
   - Category: training dynamics.
3. **Grail build refused although the engine reports it legal.**
   - 195 wasted builds; caught by the AI in a smoke game.
   - Fixed with a mask. Category: engine integration.
4. **Winner's Φ was 0, so losers were labelled 0.5.**
   - Caught by the AI in an eval smoke test before any training. Fixed. Category: reward.
5. **The headless client ended the whole game when RED lost.**
   - Caught by the AI 20 minutes into joint1. Fixed as a VCMI bug fix.
   - The AI's first version made battle-only games wait ~60 s; it caught this itself before resuming.
   - Category: engine integration.
6. **Hire exploit.** The catapult and war machines were counted as hero assets, so a hire was worth ~3,600 Φ for 2,500 gold.
   - Caught by the AI from the heroes metric: 1.5 → 3.8 per player in about an hour.
   - Fixed by unvaluing war machines; later settled at half price.
   - Category: reward. RL found the mispricing within an hour.
7. **Townless starts.**
   - The map "Knee Deep in the Dead" (9% of games, all lost on day 7) and generator template 8SM0f.
   - Fixed by removing the map and regenerating such random maps. Category: data.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Price-based value functions get exploited within an hour.**
   - Evidence: the war-machine/catapult mispricing made each hire +1,100 Φ, and heroes per player went 1.5 → 3.8 in ~1 h (10-28 00:51).
   - Lesson: audit "does buying X at list price change Φ?" for every purchasable before training. The audit built at 13:43 caught four more such mismatches on its first run.
   - Confidence: high.
2. **Measure predicted value against realized value before training.** A one-command audit comparing each action's predicted ΔΦ with the realized ΔΦ found a 3.8× underpricing of pickups and missing assets that the model would have learned around (10-28 13:43). Confidence: high.
3. **Self-play in a 4X/strategy game can settle into mutual passivity that no reward term fixes.**
   - Evidence: 2 of 1,223 and 2 of 1,253 self-play players were ever knocked out, against 70–80% elimination vs NK2. The day-28 economy improved; day-56 survival did not (10-28 06:06; 10-29 04:59, 19:04).
   - Pricing captures correctly did not create aggression.
   - Confidence: high for the observation; the remedy was unresolved in this period.
4. **In noise-dominated PPO, any small consistent push wins.**
   - Evidence: an entropy bonus (t −9..−28) and then a "floor" (t −5.8/−8.8) each drove confident shots down while the policy gradient sat at noise level (coherence 0.18–0.20 vs 0.20–0.22 noise) (10-28 09:30; 10-29 07:56).
   - Replacing a regularizer with a "safer" one moved the push rather than removing it.
   - Check every regularizer's directional effect on known-correct actions before deploying.
   - Confidence: high.
5. **Behaviours the reward barely values will drift unless something anchors them.**
   - Idle shooters cost ~−0.003 per battle overall (−0.035 per won battle) (10-29 08:03), so the total score stayed flat while the behaviour degraded.
   - Watch behaviour metrics, not just the score. The developer's "flat score + drift = real issue" was right.
   - Confidence: high.
6. **Gradient-coherence comparisons show consistency, not magnitude.** The cd reviewer's critique of the AI's first gradient study (10-28 16:21, point 4) is a general lesson for diagnosing which loss term is responsible. Confidence: medium-high.
7. **Make evals deterministic before reading trends.**
   - Before seeding, same-weight replays differed 43 vs 103 idle turns, and the AI twice read noise as a trend (10-28 04:20; 10-29 07:56).
   - Seeding the opponent from the battle state gave 198 of 200 identical replays (10-29 13:11).
   - The campaign eval at temperature 1 stayed ±0.08–0.09 per 24 games.
   - Confidence: high.
8. **Adam's per-step size caps how far any scalar can move per run.** ~1e-4 per step over 19,233 steps is ≤1.9 logits. Priors meant to be "learnable" may be frozen in practice; give them a fast reparametrization or accept that they are fixed (10-28 11:25). Confidence: high (arithmetic); the practical effect was not measured here.
9. **Short label horizons have predictable blind spots; random episode ends make a long-horizon head trainable without gaming a cutoff.**
   - Estates vs Wisdom (developer, 10-28 22:41).
   - With a fixed day-56 cap, labels in the last week degrade to same-day labels and the model can see `g_day` (10-28 14:25).
   - Confidence: medium (built; outcome not visible in this period).
10. **Overlapping multi-horizon labels: composing heads beats raw labels, but by less than intuition says.** The toy simulation gave 1.4× efficiency for week-long edges and 3–6× for edges shorter than a day (10-28 15:21). Correlated samples damage significance tests (the credit-check bug) more than the fit. Confidence: medium (toy model).
11. **Endless battles are a recurring systems risk in game-engine RL.**
    - Hit three times in 2.5 days: an eval hang of 903 s; won armies thrown away by a stalemate rule; 30–60% of campaign games silently killed for ~2 h or more.
    - Large synchronous records (0.4 MB per decision) turned a slow battle into a dead game.
    - Track game-length and kill-rate metrics per update. The AI caught the last one from "22 games averaging 18.8 days".
    - Confidence: high.
12. **Developer skepticism repeatedly found real issues behind plausible AI explanations:**
    - "how is this something that can be learned??" → the dialog confound;
    - "this sounds like it WILL explode" → unbounded features;
    - "this is not nothing" → frozen corrections;
    - "this sounds wrong!" → not the data mix but the optimizer.

    The developer was also wrong at times (the nonexistent exponent; misreading an additive prior as multiplicative). The AI handled those well by verifying and stating the evidence.

    Lesson for AI-assisted ML: AI causal claims made without a measurement should be treated as hypotheses. The most productive developer prompt was "predict failures before running" (pre-mortem, 10-28 11:57).

    Confidence: high.
13. **An extra knob changed at a restart confounds attribution.** LR 3e-5, 70% campaign battles and the endless-battle fix all landed at once (10-29 15:56); the battle gains afterwards can't be credited to any one of them (AI's own caveat, 18:43). Confidence: high.
14. **Budget numbers.**
    - A whole campaign stack (C++ AI, model, Φ, PPO, random maps) was built and running in ~1 h of AI work (10-27 14:03 → 14:53), and a joint trainer in ~45 min.
    - A 24 GB MacBook was memory-bound, not CPU-bound: swap 4–7 GB; the trainer took 12–19 GB, mostly MPS.
    - Compacting records 13× (407 → 31 KB) was the main memory fix.
    - Confidence: high.
