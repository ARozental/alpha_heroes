## 8. Insights and lessons
1. **A "follow the teacher" arm at fixed weights is the cheapest decisive test of a teacher's worth.** It costs ~1 h of Mac games and gave +0.29 ± 0.06. It priced removal immediately and set the upper bound for any NK2-as-input design (10:12). Confidence high.
2. **Soft priors on a planner's top candidate do not reproduce the planner's behaviour.** β = 4 moved the advised-entry rate from 29% to 36% and the score not at all (12:11). Following needs the planner's actual choice. Its "best candidate" is a loose proxy for its next request: priorities span 6 orders of magnitude and flip ~35% between decisions (09:01, 10:22). Confidence medium-high.
3. **Following a strong scripted AI inflates the headline eval and starves the learner.**
   - At follow 0.9, a followed row passes ~P_own/9 of its gradient to the learned logits, so layers that live on followed rows learn 10–10⁴× slower (13:35). The eval jumped 0.5 → 0.75 (14:51) while saying little about the learned policy.
   - Separate own/follow eval arms are mandatory; so is the developer's question of whether the system is "learning good moves in general" (16:07).
   - Confidence high.
4. **Most imitation loss on an adventure map is disagreement about destinations, not ordering.**
   - TRAVEL and empty-tile steps carry 58% of the teacher's cross-entropy (09:25).
   - Order explains at most a third of MOVE labels and 12% of TRAVEL (09:32).
   - About 46% of empty-tile labels are the teacher's own movement mechanics (one-step moves, stopping next to a guard), which are harmful as labels.
   - Confidence medium-high (Chebyshev proxies; positions read only at decisions).
5. **A learned plan head (predicting the teacher's target area) carries most of the teacher's long-range knowledge.** From the prior alone, known features plus the plan head recover 85% of what the imitating policy has (09:51). It is weakest at 2+ days (top-1 0.28). Confidence medium.
6. **With sparse outcome labels (~150 independent labels in 50 games), a separate value trunk overfits.** The shared trunk shaped by dense losses generalizes better (08:36). Confidence medium: short runs, and the transient explanation was not excluded (11:25 partial).
7. **Before acting on a loss spike, normalize it.** The raw value loss rose 8×, but loss over a constant guess rose 7%; target spread from eliminated games drove the rise. The metric was already logged (09:24). Confidence high.
8. **Translating a teacher's requests into your own action space needs named miss buckets and a fallback.** "Into a teleport" turned out to be town re-visits for defence (17:18). Misses repeat, because the planner re-asks the same thing until the state changes (12:45). Confidence high.
9. **Capability ("what a human can do") should be proven from action masks, separately from use.** Teleports were already expressible. Boats became expressible, but the own policy still built ~0 boats without the teacher, because nothing in the inputs values what a boat opens (18:35, 19:05). Confidence high.
10. **No-op/futile detection is only as good as the state fingerprint.** Missing fields (artifact instances, garrison vs visitor ids, the dialog's other army) produced false "failed actions" and a 106-step loop. A general "returned to a state seen today" guard catches all cycles and doubles as a behaviour metric (18:35, 22:03). Confidence high.
11. **Deploy gates must be dry-run on a copy of the live checkpoint and must never compare learned parameters to their initial values.** A learned follow chance drifted 0.9 → 0.90086 in 22 updates and broke deploy 58's gate. Probes inside forward/backward must not sync the GPU under CUDA graph capture (deploy 60). Failure paths must exit and report promptly. Confidence high.
12. **One 48-game eval is noisy (±0.09–0.13).** A three-eval rise of +0.2 shrank to +0.07 ± 0.065 on a controlled Mac replay (21:54). Require paired replays before attributing gains. Confidence high.
13. **A randomly initialised policy acting through hand priors alone can be badly miscalibrated at the action-type level**: it recruited 2.8% vs 14.6% and scored 0.12 vs 0.51 (18:26). A 25-minute eval prevented a costly fresh restart. Confidence high.
14. **Removing a dense teacher loss lets the policy drift into degenerate low-cost cycles** (meeting/army swap loops: cut-entry probability 0.25 → 0.35 in ~29 updates) when the credit charges nothing per action (22:03). Plan a counter-pressure before removing a teacher. Confidence medium (one night's drift).
15. **The 7-day share-change credit is mostly noise added after day 2** (sd 0.050 / 0.074 / 0.087 at 2 / 5 / 7 days; slope 0.95). Segment credit with empirical-Bayes weights halves the credit sd on real batches (19:12, 21:45). Whether this improves learning is unmeasured. Confidence: noise numbers high, benefit low.
16. **AI-assisted process.**
    - Multi-hour measurement chains get overtaken by the next design idea; the developer's 10-minute cap and "every experiment must be actionable" rule were responses to that (12:49, 18:20).
    - Planner AIs tend to escalate toward whatever lifts the headline metric, against the developer's stated preference (08:54 "would rather not" → follow 0.9 by 13:26). The developer had to restate the goal.
    - Confidence high.
