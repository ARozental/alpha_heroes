## 1. Summary

e5 began on 10-29 with a pure-throughput brief: "I want to train fast… if there is a tiny operation that seems unimportant but with a huge cost tell me".
- **Rounds 1–2 (Opus 5.5)** found several big hidden costs: a full game-state savegame copy on every battle decision (37% of a battle client's CPU), one map-builder process per generated battle (0.2 s each), zlib level 6 on records, and a per-hex spell-legality check (57% of encoding).
- **Exactness:** e5 verified every C++ change bit-exact, 0 of 108M and then 0 of 261M recorded values differing.
- **Merge and correction:** e5 merged on the developer's go and corrected its own overstated headline: CPU per battle decision fell 18.0 → 12.7 ms, not 27.6 → 13.4. It also capped the Mac's MPS cache and built TCP remote workers.
- **PC:** on 10-30 e5 reached the RTX 5090 PC over SSH/WSL, ran a two-machine smoke test, and found that WSL 2.7.3 kills itself 15–20 s after the last client leaves, which would have killed every run. It fixed this with a scheduled-task keep-alive.

When joint5 moved to the PC and its scores fell, e5 traced most of the drop to measurement:
- VCMI's random map generator is not deterministic, so the PC was evaluated on different maps.
- The battle-data mix had changed.
- 90% of campaign games were being thrown away.
- Four CUDA processes were time-slicing the GPU under WDDM.

The developer's architectural pushback ("why is this a config thing… a single process that has a dataloader") and "you are fable max now… Make it train fast" led to an overnight rebuild by Fable 5.1:
- DataLoader workers play games on demand, one trainer process trains both models, and inference runs off the GPU.
- CUDA-graphed campaign training, a background labeler, and passes=2.
- Result: 500–600 campaign games/h trained against 17–83 before, about 1.1M decisions/h, and the GPU at 70% mean.
- Side effects the night's checks missed: the new priority rule starved the battle learner (28–46 epochs/h, down from 90–158), and about 120 battles were silently killed. b1 found both and fixed them the next morning.

From 10-31 the session mostly turned into a reviewer and designer, with a deep audit of the model on top. On the speed side:
- **TBB bug:** VCMI's TBB thread pool spinning, about 60% of the games' CPU. Fixed by an env cap: 2.9× less CPU per turn.
- **Limits:** e5 told the developer plainly that WSL is not Linux for GPU sharing, and that no custom kernel would help.

On the model side:
- **Model gaps:** the never-learnable list (`docs/model_cannot_do.md`), option-identity hash collisions, and the teacher loss normalising over actions NK2 cannot express.
- **Battle diagnosis:** the model was "barely being trained". The remedy shipped from this, accumulate 24 → 2, was found wrong by e5 itself on 11-10.
- **Value-loss story:** an explanation the developer rejected as "half assed… excuses". The redo was mechanistic: the loss swings with batch label variance, the run was redeployed 19 times in 19.5 h against a re-fit time of about 200 updates, and the critic-trust coupling amplified each change.
- **Plans:** an optimizer plan, in which b1 caught three real errors, and plans for HotA transfer, map size (block grid) and AlphaStar/OpenAI Five lessons (privileged critic).

The period ends with Fable 5.2's audit (11-09 17:59). It found the teacher's meeting loop: 55% of MOVEs went onto our own heroes, against NK2's own 1.7%. It also found that 96.5% of the advantage's variance is shared by every decision of a player's day.

## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **Full game-state copy every battle decision.** Found by e5's profiling on 10-29. It had been there since the attack-value feature existed (unknown how long) and cost 37% of the battle client's CPU. Fix: one copy per battle with a dice reset. *Engine integration.*
2. **VCMI area-attack targets in pointer order.** Found by e5's cache check mode: 1 mismatch in 6.7k decisions. Effect: about 1 in 10,000 attack values nondeterministic, the likely cause of the ~1% of evals that don't replay. b1 fixed it in d4f95bb. *Engine integration.*
3. **Overstated perf claims.** CPU "halved" (27.6 → 13.4 ms) was measured beside joint2; the true figure is 18.0 → 12.7. e5 corrected itself 10 h later (10-30 09:40). Items 2–3 were quoted as client-process shares (corrected 14:20), and "inference 68% → legal-only heads" was wrong because features dominate (corrected 15:11). All self-detected. *Evaluation noise read as signal / process.*
4. **Mac MPS allocator hoarding.** The low watermark is 1.4 × the recommended size (24.9 GB), so swap ran at 2 GB/s. The vm_stat counters since boot suggest joint2 had run like this "for days". Mitigated with watermark 0.2. *Infra.*
5. **Fogged-tile guard queries.** About 2,300 error lines per minute, 11% of a campaign client's CPU. Found by e5; b1 fixed it with a one-line `isVisible` guard. *Engine integration.*
6. **Eval maps differ per machine.** RMG is non-deterministic per seed and evals reuse each machine's cached files. Found by e5 on 10-30 21:03 after the developer asked why scores fell. Effect: PC evals v37+ were not comparable. The developer said "ignore for now"; b1 later added `campaign_model/eval_maps/`. *Evaluation noise read as signal.*
7. **90% of campaign games dropped, 4 GPU processes time-slicing.** Found by e5. The cause was a design that grew piecemeal: fixed counts per config, push-style workers. e5 admitted "I built that part, and that was a mistake". The developer framed the fix (DataLoader pull). *Design flaw.*
8. **e5's first rebuild design put threads in the trainer** instead of DataLoader workers. The developer corrected it mid-turn (10-30 21:43). *Design flaw / process.*
9. **Training CUDA graphs failed to capture.**
   - "differentiated tensors… not used": b1's fix, `allow_unused_input=True`.
   - "AccumulateGrad stale reference": e5's own gradient check left an autograd graph alive before the first capture.
   - Fixed 10-31 ~00:15. *Infra.*
10. **Labeler bug.** A Future pickled with the game ("cannot pickle '_thread.RLock'") kept the labels in the trainer thread. Found by e5 and fixed the same night. *Infra.*
11. **memwatch kill at 23:27.** 44 loader workers plus a ~9 GB trainer. Fixed with gc.freeze, malloc_trim and the 4.5 GB floor. *Infra.*
12. **scp of two `train.py` files to one directory** overwrote the battle trainer with the campaign one, so the run failed to start. e5's own error, 10-30 night. *Infra/deploy.*
13. **Stale SSH tunnel after restart.** It held ports 7700/7800, so every PC game failed for minutes. Fixed in mac_slots.sh by e5 and then b1. *Infra.*
14. **Battle learner starvation.** Under passes=2 the "turns" rule ran battle updates only when the campaign had nothing ready, which cut epochs/h to 28–46 from 90–158. e5's design; e5 noticed it only in the next morning's table (~7 h undetected) while it had reported GPU % and the campaign rate. b1 fixed the rule. *Design flaw.*
15. **"~0 game failures" was false.** headless.sh killed silent clients at 60 s, about 120 battles in 5 h. e5 counted only logged failures. b1 found it (10-31 09:11). *Infra / monitoring gap.*
16. **Campaign eval 53 = 0.000.** 48 empty records were scored as losses and reset the adaptive teacher weight. Found by e5 (11-01 review). *Evaluation.*
17. **False "dead code".** e5's 11-01 review called seven `battle_model/features.py` helpers, `check_reach.py` and the `model.py` file deletable on a name-grep basis ("nothing references it means no file at all"). The helpers are registered by the `@stack_feature` decorator and are battle-model inputs, so deleting one breaks the trained checkpoint. b1 caught it by verifying before deleting. *Process.*
18. **TBB thread spin.** ~60% of games' CPU on the PC (13–14 workers per process, 24 hardware threads). Found by e5 on 11-02; it had been present since the PC runs began. Fixed by an env cap. *Engine integration.*
19. **Option-identity hash collisions.** 546 of 954 options share a row; same class as the shared YES token. Found by e5 on 11-04 in review. b1 fixed it with tied tables, and in doing so found the Tokenizer down-bias that made all keys cos ≈ 0.997. *Feature/encoding.*
20. **GATHER accept set turned the teacher off where GATHER was legal**, which emptied towns (eval collapse). e5 and b1 identified it independently on 11-05 10:37 (69d0332). *Reward/credit.*
21. **Teacher loss normalised over inexpressible actions.** e5's diagnosis (11-05 15:31) was partly wrong: "NK2 casts no adventure spells" is false, since Match.cpp maps CastAdvSpell to CAST (b1's verification).
   - Worse, e5's "order inside a turn must not matter" family split, implemented as 0d54e0f, drifted the policy within 3 updates: heroes left towns before buying, because NK2 buys first so troops go with the hero. b1 corrected it in 1463891 (35 min later).
   - The follow-on MULTI-out-of-span change let DROP disbands double to ~12k gold destroyed per game, fixed with disband_cost and the developer's "big minus".
   - e5 had also argued against b1's "disband can't be undone" objection and proposed DROP_FOREIGN (11-05 11:25). *Reward/credit; design flaw.*
22. **Battle model "barely trained."** The diagnosis was right: KL 5e-5, a frozen eval. The prescribed remedy (accumulate 24 → 2) was deployed by b1 at lr 1e-5 (8797125). e5 itself found it wrong on 11-10: signal 0.13 is below the pure-noise median of 0.155, so the trunk was doing a noise walk (entropy 1.01 → 0.76). *Training dynamics.*
23. **Value-loss narrative.** The first two answers (11-05 18:29, 11-06 07:33) explained without predicting; the developer rejected them. The real story: the metric is white noise from batch spread, there were too many deploys, and the 03:29 rollback was to a noisy max. *Evaluation noise read as signal; process.*
24. **Optimizer plan errors**, caught by b1 before deploy: the critic kept under the KL-driven lr_scale; a signal-ratio pass criterion that reads a working β1 change as failure; a per-group clip that would jump the gradient scale ~25× (Adam's second-moment transient, which e5 called "my worst error"); head weights still swinging with batch spread. Also the "clip at 20" proposal, retracted at the developer's push. *Training dynamics.*
25. **Stale re-proposal.** At 11-03 07:30 e5 proposed in-trainer side-stream GPU inference, which it had measured as 12× slower on 10-30. The compactions had dropped the result; e5 retracted it 13 min later after reading its memory note. *Process.*
26. **Teacher meeting loop.** NK2's first request stays "meet own hero" after the meeting, so 55% of MOVEs went onto our heroes and exchanges are 30% of decisions. It had run since at least v1322 with no failure metric. Found by Fable 5.2 on 11-09. *Reward/credit (teacher integration).*
27. **Unanswered question.** The developer asked at 10-30 22:43 why GPU memory was near full. e5 changed code (allocator logging) but gave no explanation. *Communication.*

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Profile first: the big costs in engine integration are mundane.** A per-decision savegame copy (37%), per-battle process spawn, zlib level 6 (38%), per-hex legality checks (57% of encoding) and spinning TBB workers (~60% of game CPU) together outweighed anything model-side. Each one was found by profiling, not by reasoning. Evidence: 10-29 23:59, 10-30 13:59, 11-02 20:46. Confidence: high.
2. **On a cheap single-GPU box under Windows/WSL, GPU sharing is the trap.** A second CUDA process cost the trainer 40–54% (WDDM time-slicing), and an inference thread in the trainer was 12× slower (GIL). nvidia-smi showed 91% "busy" at 186 W. Keep the GPU to one process; put small-model inference on CPU or use native Linux. Evidence: 10-30 21:03, 22:29; 11-02 21:09. Confidence: high (measured).
3. **Judge throughput by work done, not by utilisation readings.** Micro-batch 256 read 94% GPU while 1.5× slower per decision. The TB "games" scalar was games per update, not per hour. Evidence: 10-31 01:51, 08:59. Confidence: high.
4. **Supply should follow learner demand.** Hand-tuned per-machine game counts broke when the hardware ratio moved 10× (90% of games dropped). Demand-driven permits gave roughly an order of magnitude more trained campaign games per hour (500–600 against 17–83; the old figure includes restart losses). But a priority rule between two learners needs its own metric, or one learner starves. Evidence: 10-30 21:11; 10-31 08:59. Confidence: high.
5. **For strategy-game self-play with a per-decision model call, the binding resource is CPU inference latency at batch ≈1, then host RAM per in-flight game.** The engine and the GPU trainer were not the limit: ~1,500 tokens and 1.7 MB per decision, 26 ms of a PC core, 24 games capped by 27 GB of RAM. A cheaper per-decision forward (window budget, per-turn map cache) multiplies game throughput directly. Evidence: 10-31 12:57; 11-02 20:47. Confidence: medium-high.
6. **"No behaviour change" engine edits need a bit-exact replay gate.** The gate caught a real VCMI nondeterminism (pointer-ordered targets) and certified 261M values. Evidence: 10-30 08:21, 13:59. Confidence: high.
7. **Random map generators can be non-deterministic per seed. Pin the eval maps as files, never by seed, before comparing machines or runs.** Evidence: 10-30 21:03. Confidence: high.
8. **Per-batch excess losses on ~20 games are dominated by batch label variance.** Normalise them ("unexplained") before reading trends. Never roll back on one eval (SE 0.05–0.06); a rollback to the noisy max guarantees regression to the mean. Evidence: 11-06 07:44. Confidence: high.
9. **Change rate vs adaptation time.** If the critic needs ~200 updates to re-fit after a reward or label change, deploying every ~13 updates keeps the run in permanent transient. A trust-by-EV credit coupling then turns this into policy noise. Compare the deploy interval with the adaptation time before deploying. Evidence: 11-06 07:44. Confidence: medium-high.
10. **Under Adam, gradient noise matters more than step count.** With signal at or below the pure-noise median, more optimizer steps produce a weight random walk, not learning: the battle remedy failed this way (11-05 → 11-10). Measure signal fraction against the noise floor before scaling steps; verify a recipe's own stated target (KL, signal) within ~20 updates. Confidence: high (this period plus the 11-10 follow-up).
11. **Imitating a scripted AI's first request.**
    - The teacher's support must match what it can express, or every inexpressible action is pushed toward zero.
    - The scripted planner's state must reflect actions taken "our way", or its first request loops: the meeting loop drove 55% of MOVEs.
    - Accept sets let the model satisfy the teacher with one favoured entry (GATHER).
    - "Order within a turn doesn't matter" is false where order changes outcomes (buy before leaving town).
    - Evidence: 11-05 10:37, 15:31; 11-09 17:59. Confidence: high.
12. **Hashed identity tables silently collide.** 546 of 954 options shared a row. Use dense or tied tables for anything the policy must tell apart. Evidence: 11-04 19:09. Confidence: high.
13. **Lessons about the AI process itself.**
    - The AIs' first explanations were often plausible narratives. The developer's standard, "a reason that would have made you predict this in advance", reliably produced better diagnoses (11-06).
    - A second AI reviewing an implementation plan caught real errors three times (optimizer plan, dead-code list, CAST claim).
    - AI self-corrections were frequent and candid (perf numbers, item overstatements, clip 20, the γ advice).
    - Unmeasured or partly measured claims were the main error source: "~0 failures", "nothing references it", "NK2 casts no adventure spells".
    - Confidence: high.
14. **Small claims, checked before acting.** Watch for compaction-induced memory loss: e5 re-proposed an idea it had measured as 12× slower four days earlier, and caught it only via its memory note (11-03 07:43). Durable dated notes of measured negatives pay off. Confidence: medium.
