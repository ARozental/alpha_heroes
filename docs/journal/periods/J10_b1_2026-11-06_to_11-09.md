## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Have a second AI review a plan by implementing it against the code.** b1 found four errors in e5's optimizer plan; e5 confirmed three. The worst was a scale-invariance argument that ignored Adam's ~1,000-step second-moment transient (10:28, 10:49 Nov 6). *High confidence.*
2. **Adaptive-KL learning-rate control breaks when KL is batch-driven.** Per-update KL varied about 5× with the game mix and barely tracked the rate (1.14× for 1.75×). A tighter target ratcheted the rate on noise and cost about 0.08 of eval within 4 h. To slow the actor, set its rate; don't tighten the KL target (12:12, 14:50, 15:36 Nov 6). *High confidence.*
3. **Critic quality was limited by information, not optimisation.**
   - 10× critic LR: smoother fit, same 0.90 unexplained.
   - Privileged rivals' states plus true Φ: −20 to −31% error on the end-of-game head, but only −4 to −9% on 2–7-day share-change heads. Most short-horizon variance looks like future randomness.
   - Bigger batches can't help: noise = signal at ≤2,200 decisions vs 8,192 used; the residual noise is about 20 games' luck per update.
   - On a budget, more games per update or lower-variance credit beat more decisions per game.
   - *Medium-high confidence.*
4. **The developer's simpler architecture matched b1's more elaborate one** (raw max-pool vs per-rival MLP; 0fe2869 vs e9f88da). Rival data should go only to heads it helps, so check per-head benefit before applying a feature to every head (09:04–11:27 Nov 7). *Medium confidence* (different update counts, noisy).
5. **Behaviour monitors only catch failures someone has imagined.** b1 watched pickups, mines, shooters and escapes, while 55% of MOVEs were wasted on looped own-hero meetings. A fresh-eyes audit that compares behaviour distributions with a reference player (our own-hero MOVEs 55% vs NK2's 1.7%) found it (Nov 9). Periodic distributional comparisons against a reference agent are worth more than adding counters. *High confidence.*
6. **Validate metrics before alerting on them.** The "troops left" counter summed gives and ignored take-backs. Rare-event rates need medians or long means and alert hysteresis, or the watcher cries wolf (19:43 Nov 6; 16:14, 03:07, 18:42 Nov 7). *High confidence.*
7. **With an imitation teacher, every action-space change shifts existing labels.** Schema 16's new swap option became the teacher's "nearest" label for a hero's last stack, and an immediate regression followed (eval 0.335). Measure new-action rates in the first spooled games and diff the label mix before deploying (00:09–02:10 Nov 8). *High confidence.*
8. **A teacher loss normalised over an action family can suppress actions the teacher performs elsewhere.** NK2 upgrades inside town visits the matcher never sees, so UPGRADE was pushed down by RECRUIT and BUILD labels for about 2,000 updates (11:27 Nov 8). *Medium confidence* (diagnosis, not yet fixed).
9. **A few human games are a cheap audit of action-space completeness and of model gaps.**
   - Three games exposed 56 unmatched army and garrison requests, a per-tile GUI MOVE artifact (1,589 requests from 236 clicks), and 0% agreement on ARMY, UPGRADE and MULTI.
   - As imitation data they are tiny (660 decisions ≈ 1.3% of one update).
   - Build the recorder early, and normalise GUI request streams into decision-level actions (21:08 Nov 7; 11:10 Nov 8).
   - *High confidence.*
