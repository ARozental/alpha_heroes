## 1. Summary

The period opens with the battle model at contract v14 just wired into actor-based self-play training (generated battles + real H3 maps where Nullkiller2 plays the adventure map and our AlphaBattleAI plays every battle), evaluated against BattleAI and MMAI (an ML combat AI from vcmi-gym). On 10-25 22:20 the developer ordered an 11-hour run ("find the best recipe for it and go"). `v14-11h` went from TOTAL −0.038 to +0.044 (score = our reward minus the opponent's own reward in the same seat, roughly a share of army value). On the way the AI found and fixed a silent MMAI→BattleAI fallback (race on a shared VCMI config), two engine crashes, a hung macOS `sample` that froze actors for ~2 h, and a design flaw: the entropy bonus favoured action types with many sub-choices, so shooters drifted from shooting to moving (idle 0.03 → 0.53).

On 10-26 the developer pushed for speed ("I want us to be faaster with a much stronger signal") and for history inputs, and redirected the credit-assignment work several times. The AI built a central PyTorch inference server (no per-battle ONNX sessions, no per-update export), persistent VCMI battle processes and feature speed-ups: throughput rose from 73 to 238 decisions/s on the Mac. It also added contract v15 battle history (seen enemy spells, per-unit history, a 16-event log as attention tokens) and KL-controlled learning rate. The developer then set two lasting rules: LR never above 1e-4 ("I care about if learning is stable... When we go flat things are bad") and their own credit design, a linear round-based window (85% V two rounds later minus V now, plus 15% final outcome), which replaced GAE before the planned λ comparison ever ran. Six runs were started and stopped in 3.5 hours, so the final recipe was never ablated. The resulting run `v15-window` stalled for ~2 h while the value head was weak (the hero stopped casting), recovered, then went flat around +0.025.

An adversarial review by a separate session (10-27 01:13) found that after every resume, league opponents had loaded no model and only defended (~13% of generated battles). Other findings: the KL control measured against the stale behaviour policy, and actors overproduced. After cd verified and fixed these (3263898), v15-window climbed to +0.067 (update 2106), with every eval set positive against both opponents for the first time at update 1621. The developer stopped it at update 2290 on 10-27 10:19 to free the CPU for the campaign AI (b1). On 10-28/29 cd worked as reviewer and advisor. It reviewed b1's campaign AI read-only: the fight prior was far too pessimistic, the gradient study was methodologically flawed, and the defender stalemate rule was wrong. ## 4. Bugs, failures and wrong turns

1. **MMAI silently replaced by BattleAI.** Parallel MakeBattleMap runs rewrote the shared `modSettings.json`; a read of a half-written file dropped "mmai", and MMAI falls back silently. *Detected* by the AI noticing identical BattleAI/MMAI rows at eval 545 (10-26 00:18). *Undetected:* at most one eval cycle (evals 189/377 differed). *Impact:* half of one eval fake. *Fix:* read-only file for the live run; per-thread user dirs (26b150c); a guard that refuses MMAI evals without the mod (4e22867). *Category:* infra/evaluation.
2. **Real games played stale models (two bugs).** (a) Smoke: link resolved once and the file deleted after 6 updates; fixed 10-25. (b) The ONNX cache was keyed by the link path, so each real game kept its first model for 15 min while records claimed the newest version. *Detected* by the AI while building the inference server (10-26 ~10:05). *Undetected:* all of v14-11h (11 h); the "records 1.2 versions old" metric was false throughout. *Impact:* ~half of each update ~80 updates old. *Category:* infra; a metric that trusted a self-reported version.
3. **NK2 segfault (exit 139).** The first "fix" (compare hero ids) only moved the crash; the AI said so at 23:20. The AI assumed macOS throttled crash reports and ran an lldb/debug-client catcher for ~3.5 h, while the crash reports had the stack "all along" (02:26). Real cause: a data race on the hero-role table (vcmi 400b76421). *Category:* engine integration; process (wrong assumption not checked).
4. **Exit 134 abort on an unreachable move** (empty path passed the range check). Found by the crash catcher; vcmi d8c48ba91. Why our AI requested that hex: never found. *Category:* engine integration.
5. **Hung macOS `sample`** (developer mode off). Froze an eval and two real-game actors for ~2 h; the real share fell 0.50→0.33, which the AI first explained as slow NK2 turns ("isn't harmful for now", 00:53). Fixed 02:06 (66e61a7). *Category:* infra; signal misread.
6. **"Freezes" on Freedom / The Challenge.** The AI twice called them an NK2 freeze and left it ("I'm leaving it alone", 04:24; "not spending more on it tonight", 05:08). The developer insisted ("we need to find out why"). The cause was that the headless client quit only on the *red* player's win/loss; the games had ended. Engine fix 10-26 11:47. *Category:* engine integration; AI misdiagnosis corrected by developer pressure.
7. **Entropy bonus biased toward MOVE.** Whole-action entropy rewarded types with many sub-choices: eval idle 0.03 (877) → 0.53 (1242). *Detected* by the AI from the eval failure metric plus a replay test. *Undetected:* ~370 updates (~2 h). *Fix:* 0fc65e0. Later context: the drift recurred in joint1 and joint2 with other regularizers. The underlying cause (10-29): the reward barely values shots, so any systematic push wins. *Category:* design flaw / reward-credit.
8. **Worthless-cast metric** first had the defender's sign wrong, then counted every zero-value buff. Fixed (052439a). *Category:* metric.
9. **Resume renumbered models from 0**, which would have overwritten league snapshots. Found and fixed by the AI before harm (10-26 02:58). After a resume, snapshot numbers also lagged the update count ("v750" ≈ update 850; fixed 10-27). *Category:* infra.
10. **League snapshot naming (`v00050` registered, `v50` requested).** After every resume, league opponents loaded no model and only defended: ~13% of generated battles, from ~21:05 10-26 to 01:39 10-27 (~4.5 h). It coincided with the 750–860 plateau. *Detected* by the separate adversarial-review session fd6b4705, not by cd (AI: "My bug"). *Fix:* 3263898 plus `ALPHA_BATTLE_REQUIRE_MODEL` abort, so a missing model fails loudly. *Category:* infra; silent fallback.
11. **KL control flaws.** v1 raised LR 7.5× in 11 updates. v2 could raise LR above the developer's 1e-4 cap (the developer's rule exposed it). It also measured KL against the stale behaviour policy (191/811 early stops; found by the review). After the fix, 13–19% of updates still stop early on single-minibatch KL spikes while the mean update KL is ~0.005; left as "safe". *Category:* training dynamics.
12. **Actor overproduction / newest-first selection** dropped long battles (sieges) and wasted CPU of a starved learner. Found by the review; backpressure plus oldest-first. *Category:* infra / data bias.
13. **Corrupt action indices in training batches** (30570, then 10640) on MPS. Record validation did not stop the second, so the fault was not in the records. Root cause unknown (swap hypothesis); a device-side guard drops the batch. *Category:* infra (unresolved).
14. **Lid sleep.** It killed 8 eval battles, and a 2.3 h sleep would have counted as training time (budget, LR decay, eval timer). Monotonic timers (9071945). *Category:* infra.
15. **Self-inflicted crashes and restarts.** An in-place `headless.sh` edit broke running games. Moving the live TensorBoard event file crashed the trainer (AI: "That was a mistake"). v15-window had 5 resumes in one day, giving many short TB lines and missing eval points. The developer objected. *Category:* process.
16. **Idle-shooter metric** counted legitimate waits beyond 10 hexes (the developer caught it, 00:36) and wait-then-shoot (the review caught it). *Category:* metric / game knowledge.
17. **`wipe_chance` assumed uniform damage** while VCMI averages up to 10 rolls (review #5). *Category:* feature.

## 6. AI-to-AI coordination and working practices

- **Adversarial review by a separate session (fd6b4705, max effort, 10-27 00:51 → ~01:13).** The developer asked it for "actual bugs, issues that are a result of not understanding the game ... places where we are working suboptimally". It found 15 items ranked Verified/Code/Estimate. cd verified each in code and data, confirmed 13 and pushed back on 2: the reviewer said v14-11h also had the league bug (it used ONNX files), and suggested judging the worst set by a lower confidence bound (bigger sets are the fix). cd also found two more bugs while fixing. This was the single most valuable practice of the period: a fresh reader found a critical silent bug that hours of cd's monitoring missed.
- **cd as reviewer of b1 (10-28).** Strictly read-only, output pasted to the other AI. It computed claims with the model's own functions (the fight-prior table) instead of reasoning abstractly. It flagged that b1's evidence for B1 (the gradient study) did not support the claim.
- **Shared files with a live agent (10-28 17:33).** cd noticed another agent editing `constants.py`/`schema.py`, read its diff, and deliberately changed no code, handing over a checklist instead. At 22:56 it checked that the other agent hadn't touched `losses.py` for 5 h before editing comments.
- **Cross-session misinformation through memory.** cd's stale `v15-history.md` note produced the false "λ 0.8" claim in another session. Memory notes are a coordination channel and need the same correctness discipline as code.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor watches on the training log (eval totals, tracebacks, failures, later "corrupt|unsound|no model named"), re-armed every 30 min. That produced dozens of re-arm messages; a monitor window sometimes fell between evals ("no events"). Filters were narrowed over time to cut noise.
- **Testing practices that helped:** a test that fails on the old code (entropy fix); bit-identity checks for rewrites (distance maps on 2145 positions, attention bias outputs, server vs ONNX records within 4e-6, wipe_chance vs simulation); the eval-set hash kept so old evals stay comparable; test count grew 122 → 132 (211 including campaign by 10-28).
- **Diagnosis by replay:** saving eval models and replaying the same battles separated learned behaviour from bugs (850 vs 1200) and reproduced the endless battle.
- **Fail-loud guards** replaced silent fallbacks: MMAI guard, REQUIRE_MODEL abort, record validation, corrupt-minibatch guard, eval skip after two failures.
- **Practices that hurt:** in-place edits of live scripts; moving a live TB file; frequent mid-run resumes; acting on single ~128-battle evals across differing configs; announcing "best so far" for +0.005–0.01 moves (within the later-measured per-set SE of 0.02–0.04); leaving rare failures alone until the developer insisted.

## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Silent fallbacks are the costliest bug class in a multi-process RL rig.** Three cases this period: MMAI quietly played as BattleAI; league opponents with no model quietly defended (~13% of data for ~4.5 h); real games quietly kept a stale model while labelling records with the newest version (~half the data for 11 h). In none of these did anything crash. Make every fallback fail loudly, and cross-check self-reported metadata (model version) against ground truth. Evidence 10-26 00:21, 10:05; 10-27 01:15. Confidence: high.
2. **A fresh adversarial reviewer finds what the monitoring agent can't.** The separate max-effort review found the critical league bug, the stale-anchor KL flaw and overproduction in ~20 minutes. cd had been watching the same run for hours, and the 750–860 plateau ended once the fixes went in. Verifying the reviewer's claims (13/15 held) mattered too. Evidence 10-27 01:15, 08:55. Confidence: high.
3. **Under a terminal army-value reward, tactically "obvious" actions can carry almost no gradient, and any systematic push then drifts them.** In H3, once you win, the enemy army is lost whether you shot it or not. Shots pay only through prevented losses. Three drifts in 4 days (entropy bonus; joint1; joint2) with flat scores (2840 vs 3201: +0.077 vs +0.078). Remedies: a fixed prior or richer intermediate reward for such actions, or a lower-variance target (search). A stronger regularizer alone isn't one. Evidence 10-26 03:00; 10-29 07:47. Confidence: medium-high (mechanism measured; the specific push in joint2 unverified).
4. **Audit any non-zero-mean term in the objective.** The entropy bonus was ~5% of an SD per decision but systematic, and it beat the zero-mean advantage noise within ~1,000 updates. Entropy summed over hierarchical sub-choices favours actions with more sub-options (MOVE ~100 hexes vs SHOOT a few). Evidence 10-26 03:00, 09:15. Confidence: high.
5. **Credit horizons should be in game time (rounds), not decisions.** A round holds 1 to 10+ decisions depending on army size and waits, so a per-decision λ gives arbitrary horizons. The developer's game knowledge drove this, and the window credit worked in v15-window. But with 85% trust in V, the policy unlearned casting while V's EV was ≤0.2: mana is booked immediately, the benefit is invisible to a weak critic. Tie trust to critic quality. Evidence 10-26 16:41, 21:39, 22:13; 10-27 00:45. Confidence: medium (no ablation vs GAE).
6. **On a budget, inference placement dominates throughput.** Per-game ONNX on one CPU thread plus per-update export were half the actor cost. A central batched server, persistent game processes, and fixing one O(n²) feature (distance maps, half the model's compute) gave 3.3× on the same laptop (73→238 dec/s). Measure before tuning: the 512-minibatch "8×" was 1.5× on MPS, and the bottleneck claim flipped twice. Evidence 10-26 09:46–13:11. Confidence: high.
7. **Eval noise must be measured before evals drive decisions.** ~128 greedy battles per opponent; per-set SE turned out 0.02–0.04. MMAI was stochastic (temperature 0.5, random seed); BattleAI is clock-seeded; an 8-battle castle set dominated "worst set". Decisions on 10-26 (LR, credit, restarts) rested on single ~30-min evals across differing configs. Evidence 10-26 09:15, 13:53, 17:26; 10-27 01:15, 08:55. Confidence: high.
8. **One controlled comparison beats three half-runs.** The λ test was set up, preempted by restarts (LR too slow, too fast, controller bug), then dropped when the developer asked for history plus a new credit at once. The final recipe (history + window + KL control + bigger updates + hard scenarios) was never ablated, so no single gain can be attributed. Evidence 10-26 13:11–16:44. Confidence: high.
9. **Engine integration failures hide as "rare freezes".** Both "NK2 freezes" were our own headless-quit condition (red player only). The macOS debugger and `sample` hangs came from disabled developer mode, and "macOS throttles crash reports" was false. Check the platform's own diagnostics first. Evidence 10-26 02:26, 05:18, 11:47. Confidence: high.
