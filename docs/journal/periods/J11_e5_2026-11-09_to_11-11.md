## 8. Insights and lessons

1. **Audit the teacher's behaviour against itself before trusting its labels.** Our policy made 55% meeting moves while real NK2 made 1.7%. The gap came from per-decision stateless re-planning plus an NK2 bug that real NK2 also has (10 useless passes a turn). Comparing distributions (ours, the teacher's advice, the teacher playing alone) found it in an evening. Evidence: 11-09 18:50. Confidence high.
2. **Removing waste is not gaining strength.** Meeting fixes cut meeting movement from 38% to ~6% and eval time by 40%, but the score held at ~0.42. The freed movement went to END (in-town idle 49% → 70%), because a 7-day credit shared across ~25 same-day decisions (96.5% between-day variance) cannot reward a small visit over ending the turn. Evidence: 00:24, 03:10. Confidence medium-high.
3. **A new capability that raises the stakes exposes a latent unpunished failure.** Heroes died in the field just as often before GATHER, but carried ~5k. GATHER moved 20–43k onto them and players went out. The pre-test that would have caught it is cheap: the frequency of the failure times the stake the change hands over, or play the new code on a checkpoint that already uses the capability (the v2830 pre-test at 02:08 did exactly this). This was the second GATHER collapse (11-05 was the first). Evidence: 18:45, 02:08. Confidence high.
4. **The fresh value loss is the earliest collapse alarm.** It rose 9 updates after the GATHER deploy, five evals before anyone looked. A median-of-7 ≥ 3× guard fires exactly on the two collapses in 2,837 updates. Put it in the trainer, not in an external watcher. Evidence: 18:45. Confidence high.
5. **Never ship a label change just before losing observability.** The GATHER deploy (08:48) preceded the network loss (~10:10) and a ~9-hour unmonitored collapse. Off-machine watchers die with the link. Confidence high.
6. **Single evals mislead; the noise must be calibrated.** A 48-game PC eval is ±0.11 at 2 se. e5 twice built a "slide" story from 3–4 single evals; both died under a same-code Mac replay with replicates. Null pairs on the Mac also exceed their paired se (3 of them beyond 1.5 se), so arms are compared side by side with ≥2–3 replicates. The map generator was nondeterministic for a given seed, which silently unpaired tests. Evidence: 22:44, 01:20, 06:07, 19:59. Confidence high.
7. **Check the gradient-signal instrument against pure noise for the exact recipe.** With 4 passes over one batch, |m|/√v reads ~0.31 on noise, so the logged "0.25" meant nothing. The true signal share, measured across independent updates' Adam moments, was ~0.5%. A wrong instrument misled the battle work for 10 days (since Oct 31). Evidence: 10:17. Confidence high.
8. **In battle RL, weight per battle, not per decision.** Long stand-offs (27–51 rounds of WAIT/DEFEND) dominated the gradient: the value head effectively fitted ~4 battles an update, which explains its ±10% checkpoint jitter. A 1/(1+(n−1)/30) weight doubled the effective battles offline (deployed: 66 against 48, value-loss spread −57%). Evidence: 10:23, 06:31. Confidence medium; early data.
9. **"An explanation must predict the failure" works as a forcing function on AI analysts.** It made e5 run the decisive tests (fixed-battle scoring, checkpoint moments). Those tests refuted e5's own first two explanations, and e5 retracted them unprompted. Evidence: 08:08 → 10:17. Confidence high.
10. **Learnable gains on hand priors drift toward the teacher, not toward reward.** At a 7-day horizon, reward gave `night_gain` no sign (+0.00002 ± 0.00022) while the imitation loss pushed it down in 21 of 23 updates. Freeze such scalars, or learn them through an auxiliary loss on real outcomes. Evidence: 23:01. Confidence medium.
11. **A prior changes the states the teacher is asked about.** Once our heroes sheltered, NK2's labels sat on moves our prior penalises 6.7% of the time against our 3.2% (before: 0.9% against 1.3%). Imitation then pulls the base policy riskier (0.07 → 0.13), while the prior's own effect stays constant (+0.22). Mixing hand priors with imitation of a different AI creates a slow tug of war. Evidence: 05:38–05:55. Confidence medium.
12. **Units for value priors are hard on variable-size maps.** Gold through a log squash is unbounded (1M → 8.5 logits). A share of Φ under-prices late losses by ~4× by day 56 (−0.061 paired). Measure the prior's logit spread on large maps before choosing a unit. Evidence: 23:59–00:45. Confidence medium.
13. **"Decide, don't defer" has a failure mode.** After being told to stop handing over non-tradeoffs, e5 ruled b1's GATHER deferral "not a real tradeoff" (20:32), having itself called it "the one real tradeoff" 100 minutes earlier and knowing 11-05's emptied-towns collapse. It then collapsed the run. The rule removes friction but can strip out a genuine risk flag. Interpretation; confidence medium.
14. **Peer AI review pays off when each side measures rather than asserts.** Most serious errors this period were caught by the other session within an hour (the unpaired test, the double count, the biased guard, the false premise). Confidence high.
15. **Cheap local paired evals change the cadence.** 48 pinned games per arm took ~11 minutes for a pair of arms (Mac, BattleAI). That allowed a pre-test for every behaviour change and cut the per-change wait from 3 PC evals (~4 h) to 1. Caveat: Mac load may bias arms, and the Mac uses BattleAI, not our battle model. Confidence medium.
